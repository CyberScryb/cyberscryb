How to Get AI to Write Like You (Instead of Like a Robot)

AI writes like a corporate press release had a baby with a textbook. Here's why that happens, and the three moves that kill the robotic tone for good.

By CyberScryb 📅 Updated October 31, 2026 ⏱ 4 min read
⚡ Key Takeaways (TL;DR)

AI defaults to average because average is the safest prediction. Feed it a sample of your writing, ban the tells (em-dashes, delve, it's-not-X-it's-Y), and edit the result like you mean it. The draft is the machine's. The voice is yours.

The uncomfortable truth

Here's the uncomfortable truth: AI writes like everyone and no one. It was trained to produce the most average, inoffensive version of every sentence, because average is the safest prediction. Your voice lives in the specifics: the short sentences, the weird opinions, the way you'd actually say it out loud. The machine sanded all of that off by default. You have to put it back.

Move 1: Feed it your voice

Don't describe your style. Show it. Paste in a paragraph you actually wrote, something with your rhythm in it, and say: "Match this tone. Short sentences. Direct. A little funny." That one move does more than any amount of adjectives. The AI is a mimic. Give it something worth mimicking instead of letting it default to corporate.

Move 2: Ban the tells

AI has tics, and once you see them you can't unsee them. Em-dashes in every paragraph. The word "delve." The "it's not X, it's Y" construction. "Tapestry" and "landscape" as metaphors for literally anything. "In today's fast-paced world." Tell it what to avoid, explicitly: "No em-dashes. No delve. Short paragraphs." Banning five tells removes about eighty percent of the robot.

Move 3: Edit like you mean it

This is the one everyone skips, and it's the whole game. Read the draft out loud. Every sentence that sounds like a press release gets rewritten. Every paragraph that's exactly the same length as the last one gets broken up. Add one opinion the AI would never have. The draft took ten seconds. Spend five minutes making it yours. That ratio is what separates "AI wrote this" from "a person wrote this fast."

The mirror

Nobody's asking you to hide that you used AI. They're asking you to sound like yourself. The machine gives you speed. You give it spine. Feed it your voice, ban the tells, edit hard, and what comes out the other end won't sound like a robot. It'll sound like you on a good day.

FAQ

Why does AI writing all sound the same?

Because it was trained to produce the most average, inoffensive version of every sentence. It defaults to corporate-polite because that's the safest prediction. Your voice lives in the specifics it was trained to sand off.

Can AI actually copy my writing style?

Close enough to be useful. Paste in something you wrote, tell it to match the tone, sentence length, and attitude, and it'll get maybe 80 percent there. The last 20 percent is your edit, and that edit is what makes it yours.

What are the biggest AI writing tells?

Em-dashes everywhere, the phrase delve into, it's not X it's Y constructions, tapestry and landscape as metaphors, in today's fast-paced world, and every paragraph being exactly the same length. Kill those and you've removed most of the robot.

Is it cheating to use AI and then edit it?

No. That's called writing. Every professional writer drafts fast and edits hard. AI just made the draft phase faster. The thinking, the opinions, and the final call are still yours.

How to Write a Eulogy

A speaking-ready structure for the hardest speech you will give — plus how to rehearse it so the room hears what you mean.

By CyberScryb 📅 Updated February 12, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read
⚡ Key Takeaways (TL;DR)

A strong eulogy runs 3-5 minutes and follows four parts: a brief opening naming who you are, one or two specific stories, the turn toward what they meant, and a short closing farewell. Write for the voice — short sentences, natural pauses — and rehearse it aloud twice standing up.

The Four-Part Structure That Works

Every memorable eulogy follows the same bones: (1) Opening — two sentences on who you are and your relationship ("I’m David, Margaret’s son"); (2) The stories — one or two specific memories, told simply, with sensory detail; (3) The turn — what those stories reveal about who they were and what they taught you; (4) The closing — a farewell, a thank-you, or a line they would have liked. Four parts, three to five minutes, done.

Stories Beat Summaries Every Time

The instinct is to summarize a life: "She was kind, generous, and loved her family." The room already knows that — they came because it is true. What they need is the moment: the Sunday phone calls, the driveway oil change, the laugh that filled the room. One concrete story does more work than a paragraph of adjectives. Pick the story you would tell a stranger to explain who they were.

Write for the Voice, Not the Page

A eulogy is heard, not read. Short sentences. Plain words. Natural pauses where you can breathe. Read every draft aloud and cut any sentence you stumble over — grief already makes speaking hard, so the text should make it easier. Mark two or three pause points in the margin. Bring the printed page; nobody expects you to memorize this, and holding it gives your hands something to do.

What to Avoid

  • Inside jokes only three people understand — the room should feel included
  • Anything you would not say with their mother in the front row
  • Apologizing for crying or for being a bad speaker — just pause and continue
  • Going over five minutes unless the family asked for longer
  • Reading someone else’s generic eulogy off the internet — the room can tell

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a eulogy be?

Three to five minutes read aloud — roughly 400 to 700 words. Shorter and specific beats longer and general.

Is it okay to cry during a eulogy?

Yes. The room expects it. Pause, breathe, and continue — nobody will think less of you. Having water nearby and a printed copy helps.

Can a eulogy include humor?

Gentle humor that fits the person is welcome — a funny story they loved telling is often the most moving part. Keep it affectionate, never a roast.

What if I am too upset to speak?

Ask someone to stand with you, or have a backup reader ready. There is no shame in handing the page to someone you trust.

Medical Billing Dispute Kit: From Wrong Bill to Written Dispute

Hospital bills are wrong often enough that you should never pay one on sight. This kit walks you through the four steps that actually work: document it, check it, dispute it in writing, mail it right. The tools pass your facts forward so you type everything once.

Step 1: Document the bill before you touch it

Do not call the billing department yet. Do not pay anything yet. First, write down exactly what you have: the provider name, the dates of service, the total, what insurance already paid, and what they say you owe. Then log every interaction from here on. Billing disputes take months, and the person with the dated record wins.

Open the Paper Trail Timeline and add the bill as your first event. Every call to billing, every promise of a callback, every letter you send goes in as its own dated entry. When you are done here, hit the Send to buttons on the timeline page and your whole record carries into the dispute tools. No retyping.

Step 2: Demand the itemized statement and check it for errors

The summary bill they sent is not enough to pay from. Call or write and demand the itemized statement with procedure codes. Then read it line by line against what actually happened:

  • Duplicate charges. The same procedure listed twice for one visit.
  • Services you never received. Compare against your own memory and any visit notes.
  • Wrong codes. A procedure coded as something more expensive than what was done.
  • Providers you never saw. Common with ER visits and surgeries.
  • Balance billing after an emergency. The No Surprises Act bans most of this. If an out-of-network charge appears from a visit where you had no choice of provider, flag it.
  • Bad math. Add up the patient-responsibility column yourself.

Log what you find in the timeline as new events. If the bill is correct but you cannot pay it, that is a different problem: skip to the hardship note at the bottom of this page.

Step 3: Dispute it in writing

Phone calls do not count as disputes. A written dispute does. Open the Medical Debt Dispute tool. If you came from the timeline, your record is already filled in. If not, paste your timeline summary into the details box.

The tool builds a letter that demands validation of the debt, lists the specific errors you found, and tells them to stop reporting the disputed amount. Read the draft before it goes out. Fix anything it got wrong. Add anything only you know, like the name of the person who promised you a corrected bill on a specific date.

Step 4: Mail it right and calendar the follow-up

A dispute letter you cannot prove you sent is a story, not a record. Do this:

  1. Send it certified mail, return receipt requested. Keep the receipt with the tracking number.
  2. Keep a copy of everything. The letter, the bill, the itemized statement, the certified receipt. One folder, physical or digital.
  3. Log the mailing in your timeline as a new event with the date and tracking number.
  4. Calendar 30 days out. If a collector is involved, they must validate the debt or stop. If the provider has not responded in 30 days, follow up in writing referencing your first letter.
  5. Do not pay the disputed portion while the dispute is open. If part of the bill is correct, you can pay that part and say so in the letter.

If the bill is right and you just cannot pay it

Then this is not a dispute, it is a hardship case, and the move is different. Ask the provider about financial assistance or charity care first. Most nonprofit hospitals are required to have a policy. Then use the Hardship Letter tool to request a payment plan or reduction in writing. Your timeline still helps: it shows every call where you tried to resolve this in good faith.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to dispute a medical bill?

There is no single federal deadline for disputing the bill itself, but timing matters everywhere else. If a collector is involved, you have 30 days from their first contact to demand debt validation in writing. Dispute early anyway: billing departments correct errors fastest in the first 60 to 90 days.

What counts as a medical billing error?

Duplicate charges, services you never received, wrong procedure or diagnosis codes, charges for a provider you never saw, balance billing that violates the No Surprises Act, and math errors. Always demand the itemized statement before paying anything.

Should I pay any of the bill while I dispute it?

Do not pay the disputed portion. Paying can be treated as accepting the charge. If part of the bill is clearly correct, pay that part, dispute the rest in writing, and say so explicitly.

Can a hospital send me to collections while I am disputing?

They can refer the account, but a collector must stop until it validates the debt after you dispute in writing within 30 days of first contact. Put your dispute in writing and keep proof you sent it.

What is the No Surprises Act and does it help me?

A federal law banning most surprise out-of-network balance billing for emergency care and for ancillary providers at in-network facilities. If your bill has out-of-network charges from a visit where you had no choice of provider, say so in your dispute.

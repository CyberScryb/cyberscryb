Key Homeowner Due Process Protections

1. Mandatory Notice & Cure Period

State statutes prohibit boards from levying fines on a first notice. HOAs must grant a statutory opportunity to cure (usually 10 to 30 days) and specify the exact covenant alleged to be breached.

2. Right to an Impartial Hearing

Under California Davis-Stirling § 5855, Florida FS § 720.305, and Texas Prop Code § 209.006, homeowners have a statutory right to present evidence and testimony before fines become enforceable.

3. Tolling of Fines Pending Review

Timely submission of this formal written dispute letter pauses (tolls) further fines, interest, or collection referrals until the Board reviews your submission and renders a formal ruling.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the selective enforcement defense work?

If your HOA fines you for something that numerous other homeowners in the community are actively doing without penalty, the association is engaging in selective and arbitrary enforcement. Taking dated photographs of identical conditions throughout the community establishes an affirmative defense under common law and association fiduciary duties.

What happens if the board ignores my dispute letter?

By sending this letter via Certified Mail with Return Receipt Requested, you create an unassailable legal record. If the board attempts to assess fines or record an assessment lien without providing the requested hearing, the association violates statutory due process, which can render the fine void and expose the association to liability for homeowner legal fees.

Should I attend the board hearing in person?

Yes. Always attend the scheduled hearing (or request a video conference option if available). Bring two printed copies of your dispute letter, dated photographs showing compliance, contractor receipts, and notes on who you spoke with. Remain calm, professional, and factual.

Key Legal Protections Against Medical Debt

1. FDCPA § 809 Cease & Validate

When sent to collections, submitting a written dispute within 30 days legally prohibits the agency from calling you, adding collection fees, or reporting to credit bureaus until they substantiate the debt from the original hospital.

2. The No Surprises Act

Federal law bans surprise balance billing for emergency services and non-emergency services provided by out-of-network clinicians at in-network facilities. Patients cannot be billed more than in-network cost-sharing amounts.

3. Credit Bureau Protections

Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion have permanently removed all paid medical debts and unpaid medical debts under $500 from credit reports. Unpaid debts over $500 cannot be reported until at least 365 days after the bill was generated.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens when I request an itemized medical bill?

Hospital summary bills only show lump-sum categories like "Pharmacy" or "Lab Services" with massive markups. Requesting a line-by-line itemized bill with standard CPT/HCPCS procedure codes forces auditors to verify each charge. It frequently cuts bills by 30% to 70% by uncovering phantom charges, billing duplicates, or coding errors.

Can medical collectors share my medical records?

Under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA), third-party debt collection agencies are strictly limited in the Protected Health Information (PHI) they may handle. Collectors may not receive detailed clinical notes, diagnoses, or treatment records without an express patient authorization, preventing predatory disclosure.

How should I mail this medical dispute letter?

Always mail disputes via USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt Requested (the green postcard or electronic receipt). This creates an irrefutable legal timestamp proving you delivered your dispute within the statutory 30-day FDCPA window, protecting your credit and consumer rights.

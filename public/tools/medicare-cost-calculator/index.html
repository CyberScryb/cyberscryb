Medicare Cost Calculator

Compare estimated total yearly cost, not just the premium. Three paths for 2027: Medicare Advantage, Original Medicare plus Medigap and Part D, and Original Medicare plus Part D with no Medigap. Free, no signup, and all math runs in your browser. 2027 figures as of October 2026.

Estimated total yearly cost

How to read this: Expected total is premiums for 12 months plus the out-of-pocket cost you entered. Worst case is premiums plus the cap where a cap exists. Path C has no medical out-of-pocket cap, so its worst case is not a finite number. Drug out-of-pocket in Paths B and C is capped at $2,400 for 2027 in this estimate, after the $700 maximum Part D deductible framework. Confirm exact drug pricing in the Medicare Plan Finder.

How to Use This Estimator

1. Start with Part B. Keep $209.50 only as the Trustees projection, or replace it with the figure on your notice. Add any IRMAA surcharge you actually pay.

2. Enter real plan prices. For Medicare Advantage, use the premium and MOOP from the plan Annual Notice of Change mailed by September 30. For Medigap and Part D, use the quotes for your ZIP code.

3. Estimate a normal year honestly. Count last year doctor and specialist visits, hospital stays, imaging, therapy, and drug spending at plan prices. Then look at the worst-case column before you decide.

4. Verify before enrolling. Check every doctor, hospital, and prescription at medicare.gov/plan-compare. This estimator does not check networks or formularies.

When Each Path Tends to Win

Medicare Advantage tends to win the expected-total column in a light-use year because the extra premium can be $0 or low. It loses predictability: copays rise with use, networks and prior authorization apply, and the worst case runs to the plan MOOP.

Original Medicare plus Medigap tends to win the worst-case column. You pay Medigap and Part D premiums every month, but a bad health year adds little beyond the Part B deductible on many popular Medigap plans. It also keeps nationwide access to providers who accept Medicare.

Original Medicare plus Part D with no Medigap can look cheap in a healthy year and turn expensive without warning. There is no cap on the 20 percent Part B coinsurance. Treat Path C as the exposure warning, not the safe default.

The 2027 Numbers Used Here

Confirmed for 2027 as of October 2026: Part D maximum deductible $700 and Part D out-of-pocket cap $2,400. Projected, not final: Part B premium $209.50 per month from the Trustees report and the first IRMAA thresholds of about $113,000 single and $226,000 married. The final 2026 first IRMAA threshold is $109,000 single and $218,000 married filing jointly. CMS publishes final Part B and IRMAA figures in November, so revisit the estimate then.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I estimate my total Medicare cost for 2027?

Add 12 months of premiums to the out-of-pocket cost you expect in a normal year, then compare that total to the worst case: premiums plus the out-of-pocket maximum where one exists.

What are the confirmed 2027 Part D numbers?

Part D maximum deductible $700 and Part D annual out-of-pocket cap $2,400. Both are final from CMS as of October 2026.

What is the 2027 Part B premium?

Not final yet. This calculator defaults to the Trustees projection of $209.50 per month and labels it as a projection. CMS confirms the official figure in November.

What are the IRMAA thresholds for 2027?

Projected at about $113,000 single and $226,000 married, pending CMS confirmation. Final 2026 first threshold: $109,000 single and $218,000 married filing jointly.

Does this calculator check doctors, drugs, or plan availability?

No. It is an estimator, not a plan finder. It does not check networks, formularies, or county availability. Verify at medicare.gov/plan-compare.

Is this Medicare calculator free and private?

Yes, free with no signup. All math runs in your browser and nothing you enter is sent to a server.

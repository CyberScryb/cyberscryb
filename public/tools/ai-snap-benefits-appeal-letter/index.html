How SNAP appeals actually work

The notice is the authority

The notice states the decision, the reason, the deadline, and where to send the fair hearing request. Read it first. This tool only does the date math from the notice date you enter; it does not set your deadline.

Two deadlines, not one

The 90-day fair hearing window is the outer deadline. The 10-day advance notice window is the one that keeps benefits going while you wait. If your benefits are being cut, the 10-day window is the one that matters first.

The evidence is the appeal

A letter that only says you disagree will not move the case. Pay stubs, rent receipts, employer letters, and proof of who lives in the household are what the hearing officer rules on. Enclose copies, keep the originals.

One clear ask

Ask for a fair hearing, and ask for benefits to continue while the appeal is pending if that applies to you. Letters that ask the agency to "make it right" get stalled. A specific request with a date forces a specific answer.

Certified mail creates the paper trail

The return receipt proves the agency got the letter and when. An email can be ignored or lost. A certified letter has a signature, and the follow-up round depends on being able to cite the first mailing by date and tracking number.

Losing can mean repaying aid pending

If your benefits continue during the appeal and you lose, the agency may ask you to repay the benefits you received while waiting. That does not mean you should skip the appeal; it means you should ask a legal aid organization in your state before you decide.

Common questions

What is a SNAP fair hearing?
A fair hearing is a review of your state agency's SNAP decision by an impartial hearing officer. You request it in writing when the agency denies, reduces, or ends your benefits, or takes too long to decide your application. The hearing officer reviews the agency's decision against the evidence you both present.
How long do I have to appeal a SNAP decision?
Federal rules generally give you 90 days from the date of the notice to request a fair hearing. But if your benefits are being reduced or ended and you want them to continue while the appeal is pending, you usually have to request the hearing within the advance notice period, generally 10 days after the notice was mailed. Read the notice you received: it states the exact deadline and where to send the request.
What is aid pending, and how do I keep my benefits during the appeal?
Aid pending means your benefits continue at the current level while the appeal is pending, usually if you request the fair hearing within the advance notice period, generally 10 days after the notice was mailed. If you lose, the agency may ask you to repay the benefits you received during the appeal. If your benefits were denied on a new application rather than cut from an existing case, there are no benefits to continue.
Where do I send a SNAP appeal letter?
To the address on your notice, which states where to send the fair hearing request. Send it certified mail with return receipt requested and keep the receipt and a copy of everything you send. The notice, not this tool, is the authority on the address and the deadline.
What should a SNAP appeal letter include?
Your name, case number, the date of the notice, what the agency decided and why it said so, what you disagree with in plain words, the facts that support your side, the evidence you are enclosing, and a clear request for a fair hearing, plus a request that benefits continue while the appeal is pending if that applies to you.
What if I miss the 90-day deadline?
Ask your state agency or a legal aid organization in your state right away. A late request may still be accepted in some situations, and you may be able to reapply for SNAP even if the appeal window has closed. Do not assume the case is over because the date passed; get advice from someone who knows your state's process.

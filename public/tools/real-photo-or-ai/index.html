Real Photo or AI

Look at one picture and say whether a camera or an AI made it. The real photos are public-domain or CC0 pictures of a forest, a cup of coffee, an alley, a fox, a coast, a loaf, a field of stars, a desk, a bee, and a barn. The AI pictures cover the same subjects and were generated for this page. A round is 10, shuffled, five of each. The reveal names the source, then one or two habits to notice. Detection is unreliable, and the page says so.

  1. Look One picture. The description does not give the answer away.
  2. Choose Real or AI. Press R or A, or use the buttons.
  3. See the source Author and license, or a line that says the picture was generated by AI for this site.

Why a tidy picture is not proof

A centered path, a heart in the foam, and a barn with even boards show up often in generated pictures. So does a sky with no dust and no streak. Cameras do all of those things too. The Hoh Trail photo is messy because a log fell there, not because mess is a seal of authenticity. The Spitzer picture is dazzling and still a real NASA exposure. The tells under each reveal are there to practice a close look. They are not a verdict you could use on someone else.

If you want a different kind of practice, the AI or Human writing test asks you to read a passage, the Prompt Battle compares two prompts without calling a model, and the AI detector reads text you paste. The detector and the AI humanizer send text to an AI. This page does not. The Tech Myth or Fact deck stays in the browser too.

FAQ

Does this upload my answers?

No. The quiz runs in your browser. The only thing saved is your best score, in this browser's local storage. The photo you saw and the choice you made are not stored and are not sent anywhere. The page does not call an AI.

Where do the real photos come from?

Each real photo is a public-domain U.S. government image or a CC0 file. The Credits section names the author, the license, and the Wikimedia Commons file page where that license is recorded. The pictures are stored with this page. Playing does not fetch them from those sites.

Were the AI photos made live by a model?

No. The AI photos were generated ahead of time for this game and saved on this site. The reveal labels them Generated by AI for this site. Playing does not send the picture to an API.

How does a round work?

Each round is 10 pictures, five real and five AI, drawn from a deck of 20 and shuffled. You choose Real or AI, then see the source and one or two tells. Play again deals another 10. Your eye accuracy is the share of the round you got right.

Are the tells reliable?

No. Even lighting, repeated shapes, and a too-clean center are habits, not proof. A camera can make a tidy picture, and a model can copy a crooked one. The note under each reveal says so.

What is saved on this device?

Only your best score. Clearing site data for cyberscryb.com removes it. The quiz does not ask for an account, and leaving the page drops the current round.

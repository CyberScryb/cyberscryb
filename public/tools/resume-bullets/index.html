Why Recruiters Skim Past Most Resumes

Recruiters give a resume about six seconds before deciding which pile it lands in. In six seconds they cannot read duties. They can only scan for proof: numbers, scope, and outcomes. A resume full of responsible for and helped with reads as a job description, not a record of results, and it loses to the candidate whose bullets answer so what in every line.

Strong bullets follow one discipline, the Google XYZ formula: accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z. The result first, the metric that proves it, the action that caused it. Most people write only the Z, the duty, and wonder why nobody calls. This tool takes your plain description of what you did and rebuilds it around X and Y, with a strong verb in front and your target role vocabulary woven in for ATS matching.

Your notes go to our backend and Google Gemini for generation; nothing here runs only in your browser, and CyberScryb does not store your resume text. Paste rough, honest notes in. Get bullets you still have to sanity-check, because a metric you cannot defend in an interview is worse than no metric at all.

How It Works

Step 1: Describe what you actually did. Informal is fine. Led the migration, trained new people, handled the angry customers. Duties, wins, and numbers all belong here.

Step 2: Add your target role. Optional but powerful. The vocabulary, emphasis, and technical depth shift to match what hiring managers and ATS systems scan for in that role.

Step 3: Generate and pick your strongest. Three to six bullets per job is the target. Keep the ones where the metric is real and the verb sounds like you on your best day.

Step 4: Verify every number. If a figure is an estimate, know your range and your reasoning before the interview. Then mirror the exact skill names from the posting you are applying to.

What makes a good resume bullet point?

Strong bullets start with an action verb, include quantified results (metrics, percentages, dollar amounts), and follow the STAR framework: Situation, Task, Action, Result.

What are the best action verbs for resumes?

Top-performing verbs include: spearheaded, optimized, engineered, orchestrated, accelerated, delivered, reduced, and increased. Our AI selects the strongest verbs for your context.

Should I include metrics in every bullet?

Whenever possible, yes. Numbers make your impact concrete. If you don't have exact figures, reasonable estimates (e.g., "~30% improvement") are better than vague claims.

Does the target role affect the output?

Yes. When you specify a target role, the AI tailors vocabulary, emphasis, and technical depth to match what hiring managers and ATS systems look for in that position.

How do I write resume bullets if I have no metrics?

Estimate honestly with scope instead of results: team size, volume, frequency, or time saved. Handled support tickets becomes Resolved 25 to 30 customer tickets per day. If you truly have no number, use a before-and-after comparison. Never invent figures you cannot defend in an interview.

What is the XYZ formula for resume bullets?

Accomplished X, measured by Y, by doing Z. It forces every bullet to carry a result, a metric, and the action that caused it, instead of a duty copied from the job posting. Reduced churn by 12 percent by launching a weekly onboarding call is XYZ in one line.

How many bullet points should each job have?

Three to six for recent roles, fewer as jobs get older. Every bullet must earn its line with a distinct result. Two strong quantified bullets beat six vague ones, and recruiters skim, so lead each job with its biggest measured win.

Do resume bullets need to start with action verbs for ATS?

Starting with a strong verb helps human readers, but ATS systems score keyword match against the job description more than verb choice. Mirror the exact skill and tool names from the posting, keep standard section headings, and never hide text in headers, tables, or graphics.

How long should a resume bullet be?

One to two lines. Long enough for the metric and the method, short enough to scan in six seconds. If a bullet needs three lines, it is probably two achievements competing. Split it.

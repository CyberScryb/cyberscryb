What actually matters

One clear reason for leaving

HR will ask why you are leaving, and the answer you give becomes the record. Pick one reason before the meeting, say it the same way every time, and do not add a second reason when the first one lands. A single clear reason sounds confident; three shifting reasons sound like a story you are still writing.

Nothing you say is off the record

HR takes notes and the interview goes on file. Assume anything you say can be repeated inside the company, and anything you say about a person can reach that person. Keep answers short and factual, give feedback about processes and patterns instead of naming people, and do not sign anything on the spot.

Rehearse the sensitive topic once, calmly

Every exit interview has one question you are dreading: the pay dispute, the manager conflict, the reason for the termination. Decide your one calm sentence about it before the meeting, say it, and stop. The mock interview room exists for exactly that sentence.

Leave on terms that protect the reference

The interview is also your last impression. A professional exit keeps your references warm and your next application clean. Ask the practical questions: final pay, benefits end date, equipment return, and who to list as a reference. Then thank them and go.

FAQ

What is an exit interview?

A short meeting with HR on or near your last day where they ask why you are leaving and what could have been better. It is your employer's record of the departure, and your chance to leave a clear, professional final impression. The mock interview room above lets you rehearse the question-and-answer rhythm before the real one. This is general information, not HR or legal advice.

Do I have to attend an exit interview?

Usually no one can force you to attend, and many people skip it. But attending with a plan is often the better move: you control the story of why you left, you can raise genuine feedback calmly, and you leave on professional terms with people who may be references. Decide before the meeting, not in it.

What should I say in an exit interview?

One clear reason for leaving, two or three calm, specific points of feedback, and practical questions about pay, benefits, and equipment return. Keep answers short and factual. Do not badmouth a manager by name, do not raise new accusations, and do not sign anything on the spot. The prep packet mode drafts your answer scripts from your facts.

What should I not say in an exit interview?

Do not vent, do not name people to blame, do not exaggerate, and do not make claims you cannot back up. Avoid anything you would not want repeated to your next employer. If a topic is sensitive, decide your one calm sentence about it before the meeting and do not go further. List those topics in the sensitive topics section above; the strategy mode plans around them.

Can what I say in an exit interview be used against me?

HR takes notes and the interview goes on file, and what you say can be repeated inside the company. That is why the tool focuses on short, factual, professional answers and on rehearsing the sensitive topics in advance. This is general information, not legal advice; if a sensitive issue is involved, review it with an employment attorney or legal aid first.

How It Works

Generating a batch of tweets takes about thirty seconds, and the whole thing happens in four steps.

  1. Type your topic or idea. One sentence is enough, though more detail gets you more specific tweets. Not sure what to write? Hit Try Sample to load a working example first.
  2. Choose how many tweets you want. Pick 3, 5, or 10. A bigger batch gives you more to choose from, which is useful when you are stocking up a content queue.
  3. Pick an angle. Hot take writes contrarian, engagement-driven posts. Educational explains a concept. Promotional sells a product or launch. Personal story frames your idea as a lived experience. The angle controls the voice and structure of every tweet in the batch.
  4. Generate, then copy and post. Hit Generate Tweets, read through the batch, and copy the ones you like straight into Twitter/X, LinkedIn, Threads, or anywhere else you post.

Real Ways People Use It

The blank compose box is the bottleneck for almost everyone who posts online. Here is how real users get past it.

  • Founders and indie hackers turn one launch announcement into a full week of posts, generating the same idea with a different angle each day so the feed never repeats itself.
  • Creators and newsletter writers repurpose a single blog post or issue into a batch of short posts that drive traffic back to the long version.
  • Freelancers and consultants post proof-of-work threads and offer announcements without hiring a ghostwriter, using the promotional angle for launches and personal story for credibility.
  • Job seekers sharpen their profile posts and hot takes on their industry so they read like a thought leader instead of a resume.
  • Side-project builders keep a consistent posting schedule during a build in public, because ten tweets a week is the difference between a launch that lands and one nobody hears about.

One tip that applies to all of these: always rewrite the keeper tweets in your own voice before posting. A thirty-second edit keeps the engagement of a strong hook while making the post sound unmistakably like you.

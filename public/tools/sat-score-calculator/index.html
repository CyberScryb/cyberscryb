How to Use the SAT Score Calculator

Enter the number of questions you got right in each section: 0 to 54 for Reading & Writing, 0 to 44 for Math. Then pick which Module 2 you saw: the harder path if you did well on Module 1, the easier path if you struggled. The result updates as you type.

The difficulty path matters a lot. A harder Module 2 can reach a full 800 with a perfect raw score; an easier Module 2 caps out around 630 in Reading & Writing and 590 in Math. That is why two students with the same number of correct answers can get different scaled scores. For the full explanation, read our guide: how digital SAT scoring works.

Worked Example

A student finishes a practice test with 42 of 54 correct in Reading & Writing and 36 of 44 correct in Math, on the harder Module 2 path for both. Her Reading & Writing raw score converts to roughly 720, and her Math raw score converts to roughly 760, for an estimated total of 1480 out of 1600. If she had seen the easier Module 2 path instead, the same raw scores would land her around 550 and 530, or about 1080 total. The path changes everything.

What the Estimate Is (and Isn't)

This calculator gives an estimate, not an official score. The real conversion is done by the College Board with a process called equating, which adjusts for the exact difficulty of the questions you saw. No third-party tool can reproduce that exactly. The tables here are modeled on the College Board's published practice-test score conversions, so they are accurate enough for practice-test scoring and study planning, but the only official score comes from the College Board.

There is no guessing penalty on the digital SAT, so your raw score is simply the number of questions answered correctly. Blank and wrong answers count the same: zero. If you used this calculator by counting your correct answers on a practice test, you are using it exactly the way it was built to be used.

The 200-800 Scale

Each section is scored from 200 to 800, and the total is the sum: 400 to 1600. Scores move in 10-point increments. The old 2400 scale (with a separate Writing section) was retired in 2016, so the SAT today is out of 1600, period.

Real Use Cases

Practice-test scoring. After each College Board practice test, plug in your raw correct counts and get a scaled score in seconds instead of hunting through conversion tables.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the digital SAT scored?

The digital SAT is section-adaptive. Each section (Reading & Writing, Math) has two modules. Everyone gets the same first module; your performance on it routes you to a harder or easier second module. Your raw score is the number of questions answered correctly (there is no penalty for wrong answers). The College Board then converts that raw score to a scaled score of 200-800 per section using a process called equating, which adjusts for the difficulty of the questions you saw.

What is a good SAT score?

The national average is around 1050-1060 out of 1600. A 1200 is roughly the 80th percentile, 1350 roughly the 90th, and 1450+ roughly the 96th. What counts as "good" depends on the school: many state schools admit students in the 1000-1200 range, while highly selective colleges typically look for 1400-1500+. Compare your score to the published ranges of your specific colleges.

How many questions can I miss and still get a 1400?

Roughly, on the harder Module 2 path: about 40 of 54 correct in Reading & Writing (missing about 14 questions) and about 29-31 of 44 correct in Math (missing about 13-15 questions), for around 700 per section. On the easier Module 2 path, 1400 is out of reach: the easier path caps around 1200-1230. These are estimates, not official numbers.

Is the SAT out of 1600 or 2400?

The SAT is out of 1600: two sections, 200-800 each. The 2400 scale with a separate Writing section was retired in 2016. Old 2400-scale scores cannot be directly compared to current scores.

What is superscoring?

Superscoring means a college takes your highest section scores across multiple test dates and combines them. If you scored 700 Reading & Writing and 600 Math in March, then 650 and 700 in May, your superscore is 1400 (700 + 700). Many colleges superscore the SAT, so a strong single section is never wasted.

The tables here are modeled on the College Board's published practice-test conversions and are meant for practice-test scoring and study planning. Only the College Board issues official scores.

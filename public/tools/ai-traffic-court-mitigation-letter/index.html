What actually matters

Admit first, ask second

Mitigation only works when the letter owns the violation in the first paragraph. Judges read hundreds of these; anything that reads like an excuse or blames the officer or the equipment gets skimmed. State it plainly: what you did, that you accept it, then the ask.

One page, one specific request

Pick the request up front: reduced fine, traffic school, or payment plan. A letter that asks for everything asks for nothing. The tool targets 200 to 300 words and frames the request the court actually grants.

The deadline is the easy part to miss

Your ticket or court notice states the response date. Miss it and the letter is moot. Enter the date above and the tool counts the days on your device, so the letter names the date precisely. It does not know court closures.

Changed behavior is your evidence

"It will not happen again" is a promise, not evidence. A completed defensive driving course, a repaired speedometer, a changed route or schedule is evidence. Put what you did after the ticket in the letter; that is what the judge remembers.

FAQ

What is a traffic court mitigation letter?

A mitigation letter means you admit the traffic violation and ask the judge for leniency: a reduced fine, traffic school, or a payment plan. It is the opposite of contesting the ticket, where you argue you are not guilty. This tool drafts the mitigation letter from your facts. It is general information, not legal advice.

What is the difference between mitigation and contesting a ticket?

Mitigation admits the violation and asks for a lighter penalty; contesting denies it and argues for dismissal. They are different lanes in most traffic courts. Picking mitigation means giving up the fight over guilt, so only choose it if you cannot honestly contest the ticket. This tool drafts mitigation letters only.

Can a mitigation letter get my ticket dismissed?

No. Mitigation asks for leniency, not dismissal, and no letter can promise any outcome. A judge may reduce the fine, grant traffic school, or set up a payment plan, or may change nothing. If you want the ticket dismissed, that is a contested hearing, a different process.

Can I use this for a reckless driving or DUI charge?

No. This tool is for routine moving violations like speeding or stop-sign tickets. Serious charges such as reckless driving, DUI, or driving on a suspended license can carry license suspension or jail time. Talk to a traffic attorney before your court date, not an AI draft.

Will traffic school keep the ticket off my record?

In many courts, completing an approved traffic school keeps the conviction off your driving record, but eligibility rules and effects vary by state and court. The letter asks the judge for permission; it does not guarantee it. Confirm the rules with your court clerk.

What should a mitigation letter include?

The case facts (ticket number, violation, date), a clear admission of responsibility, a short explanation of the circumstances, what has changed since (for example a completed driving course), one specific request (reduced fine, traffic school, or payment plan), and any supporting evidence. Keep it to one page and never blame the officer or the equipment.

How long should the letter be?

One page. Judges read dozens of these; a tight, honest, one-page letter with a specific ask beats a long story. This tool targets 200 to 300 words for the letter body.

Is this legal advice?

No. This tool drafts a mitigation letter from the facts you enter. It does not tell you whether to mitigate or contest, whether you are eligible for traffic school, or what the judge will decide. Review the draft with a traffic attorney before submitting.

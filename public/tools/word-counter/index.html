Frequently Asked Questions

How does the word counter work?

Paste or type your text and the tool instantly counts words, characters (with and without spaces), sentences, paragraphs, and estimates reading time — all processed in your browser.

Is this word counter free?

Yes, 100% free with no signup. Your text is processed entirely in your browser and never sent to any server.

How is reading time calculated?

Reading time is estimated at 200 words per minute (average adult speed). Speaking time is at 130 WPM.

Why Word Count Matters

Word count isn't just a number — it's a signal. For SEO, Google tends to rank longer, more comprehensive content higher for competitive queries. Studies consistently show that top-10 results on Google average 1,400-1,800 words. That doesn't mean longer is always better, but it means thin content (under 300 words) rarely ranks for anything competitive.

For academic writing, word count requirements exist because they correlate roughly with depth of analysis. A 500-word essay forces you to be concise. A 3,000-word paper requires you to develop arguments, engage with sources, and address counterpoints. Hitting the word count isn't the goal — covering the topic thoroughly is.

For social media and marketing, shorter is usually better. Tweet threads that go viral tend to be 5-7 tweets of 200-280 characters each. LinkedIn posts that get engagement are typically 150-300 words. Email subject lines should be under 50 characters. Knowing your word count helps you stay within the format constraints of each platform.

Reading Time and Readability

The average adult reads about 238 words per minute for non-fiction and 260 words per minute for fiction. This tool calculates estimated reading time based on these averages, which helps you gauge whether your content is appropriate for its audience and context.

Blog posts that perform best are typically 7-10 minute reads (1,600-2,400 words). Newsletter emails should be under 3 minutes (under 700 words). Product descriptions work best at 100-300 words. Landing pages vary widely, but the key sections (headline, value proposition, call to action) should be scannable in under 30 seconds.

Readability matters as much as length. A 2,000-word article with short paragraphs, subheadings, and bullet points reads faster than a 1,000-word wall of text. Break paragraphs at 3-4 sentences maximum. Use subheadings every 200-300 words. Front-load important information in each paragraph so skimmers catch the key points.

Character Counts for Different Platforms

Twitter/X: 280 characters per tweet. URLs count as 23 characters regardless of length. Images don't count toward the limit.

Google title tags: 50-60 characters before truncation. Google measures in pixels, not characters, so wide letters (W, M) may cause earlier truncation.

Google meta descriptions: 120-155 characters on desktop, 120 on mobile.

Instagram captions: 2,200 character maximum, but only the first 125 characters show before "more."

LinkedIn posts: 3,000 characters. The first 210 characters show before "see more" on mobile.

This tool tracks both word count and character count in real time, so you can write directly to these constraints without guessing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an appeal letter?

An appeal letter is a formal written request to reverse or reconsider a decision you believe is unfair or incorrect. It presents your case clearly, provides supporting evidence, and asks the decision-maker to take another look.

How quickly should I file an appeal?

Most appeals have strict deadlines — usually 10 to 30 days from the date of the denial letter. Check your denial notice for the exact deadline. Filing sooner is always better, as late appeals are often rejected automatically.

Will AI write a legally valid appeal?

This tool helps you organize your argument into a clear, professional letter. It is not a substitute for legal advice. For complex cases involving significant money or legal rights, consider consulting an attorney after using this tool to draft your initial letter.

Is my information private?

Yes. Your input is processed to generate the letter and then discarded. We do not store, share, or train on your personal information.

What if my appeal is denied?

Many decisions can be appealed multiple times at different levels. If your first appeal is denied, you may be able to escalate to a supervisor, an independent review board, or even a court. Each denial letter should explain your next options.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a formal appeal letter actually work?

Yes — especially when the original denial was based on missing documentation or a misclassification. A clear, specific letter that directly addresses the denial reason has a real chance of reversal. Institutions have formal review processes and are legally required to consider appeals in many cases.

What's the most important thing to include?

The denial reason, directly refuted. Don't write a general letter — find the exact reason they gave for the denial and argue against it specifically. Include supporting documents and reference them in the letter.

How long should an appeal letter be?

One page is ideal, two pages max. Decision-makers read hundreds of these — brevity signals that you know what matters. State your case, provide evidence, make your request. Don't pad it.

Who should I address it to?

The specific department or person named in your denial letter. If it says "contact the Claims Review Board," address it there. Generic addressing is weaker — call the office if you don't have a name.

What if my appeal is denied again?

Most institutions have multiple levels of appeal. Ask specifically about your next escalation option — a supervisor review, ombudsman, formal hearing, or regulatory complaint. Document every interaction in writing.

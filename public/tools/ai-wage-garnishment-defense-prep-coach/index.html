What actually matters

File the exemption claim before the deadline

The deadline to challenge a garnishment is printed on your garnishment papers, and it is the only date that matters first. It is short, often days, and it varies by state and court. Miss it and the withholding keeps running while you sort out the next step. File the claim with the court, send a copy to the creditor or its lawyer, and keep proof of both. The countdown box above works from the date you enter, so copy it straight off your papers and verify it with the court clerk.

Some money is protected, but you have to claim it

Head-of-household status, Social Security and VA benefits, public assistance, and hardship are the exemptions people usually claim, but the court does not apply them for you. You file the claim, you bring the proof, and you explain it at the hearing. That is why the strongest cases are usually the simplest: pay stubs that show the withholding, a budget that shows what is left, and benefit letters that show which income is protected.

Pay stubs and budgets beat memory

“I cannot afford this” persuades no one. Pay stubs that show the withholding, a written budget with rent, food, and utilities, and bank statements that show where protected money came from: those decide hearings. Gather every record before you file anything, and send new evidence to the court the way your court requires, not by guessing.

Never miss the hearing date

A no-show can end the challenge against you, and the garnishment keeps running. If the date truly does not work, contact the court clerk before the hearing, not after, and ask how to request a change. Treat the hearing date like the hearing date it is, and keep every letter and record organized while you wait.

FAQ

How much of my paycheck can be garnished?

For an ordinary creditor judgment, federal law limits garnishment to the smaller of 25 percent of your disposable earnings or the amount by which your weekly disposable earnings exceed 30 times the federal minimum wage. Child support, tax levies, and federal student loans follow different limits, and some states protect more of your pay than the federal floor. The tool checks the federal arithmetic from the pay figures you enter and tells you to verify the limit that applies to you with the court clerk or your payroll department. This is general information, not legal advice.

How do I stop a wage garnishment?

The usual route is a claim of exemption filed with the court that issued the garnishment, before the deadline printed on your garnishment papers. The claim states why some or all of the garnished pay is protected, such as head-of-household status, protected benefit income, or hardship, and the court sets a hearing. Other routes depend on your situation: paying or settling the judgment, challenging a judgment you were never served for, or a payment plan with the creditor. The prep packet mode drafts your exemption claim checklist and hearing points from your facts. This is general information, not legal advice.

What is a claim of exemption?

A claim of exemption is a written filing that tells the court some or all of the money being garnished is protected and should not be taken. You file it with the court before the deadline on your garnishment papers, send a copy to the creditor or its lawyer, and keep proof of both. The court then sets a hearing where you explain the exemption with your pay stubs, budget, and benefit letters. The deadline to file is short, so check your papers first and ask the court clerk for the form if you do not have it. This is general information, not legal advice.

Can my wages be garnished without a court judgment?

For an ordinary creditor debt, no: the creditor usually needs a court judgment first, and that means you should have been served with a lawsuit. Child support, tax debts, and federal student loans are different: those can be garnished through an administrative process without a court judgment. If you were never served for the lawsuit behind the judgment, that is something to raise with a consumer attorney or legal aid office right away, because the deadline to challenge it is short. This is general information, not legal advice.

Is Social Security income protected from garnishment?

Social Security benefits are protected from garnishment for most ordinary debts under federal law, but the protection is not automatic in every situation: child support and federal debts such as taxes and student loans can reach Social Security income, and a bank may freeze an account holding mixed funds until you prove which money is protected. If your only income is Social Security, say so in your claim of exemption and bring the benefit letter and bank statements that show where the money came from. This is general information, not legal advice.

What is the deadline to challenge a wage garnishment?

The deadline to file a claim of exemption is printed on your garnishment papers and it is short, often a matter of days from when the order was served on you or your employer. It varies by state and by court, so there is no single number this tool can give you. Read the deadline off your papers, put it in the tool, and verify it with the court clerk. Missing it usually means the garnishment keeps running while you sort out the next step. This is general information, not legal advice.

What should I bring to a garnishment hearing?

Bring the garnishment order and the judgment behind it, pay stubs showing the withholding, a household budget that shows your rent, food, utilities, and other essentials, proof of any protected income such as benefit letters or bank statements, and a copy of the claim of exemption you filed. Bring copies for the judge and the creditor's lawyer, and keep your originals. Organize it in the order the judge will ask about it: the order, your pay, your exemptions, and your budget. The prep packet turns your evidence list into a checklist. This is general information, not legal advice.

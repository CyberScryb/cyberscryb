How to Send Certified Mail for Gym Cancellation

1. Print & Sign Two Copies

Print this letter. Sign and date both copies in ink. Keep one signed copy for your permanent records alongside your membership contract.

2. USPS Certified Mail + Green Card

At the post office, request USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt Requested (PS Form 3811 green card). This provides legal proof of delivery date.

3. Stop Payment with Your Bank

If the club bills after the effective date, provide your bank with a copy of this letter and certified delivery proof under Federal Regulation E.

When to Use This Letter

Any of these is a reason to cancel in writing instead of arguing at the front desk:

  • You moved. A relocation more than 25 miles from your home club, with no affiliated club nearby, is a cancellation ground under most state health club statutes. Attach proof of your new address.
  • A doctor says you cannot use the gym. A medical waiver ends the membership when a licensed provider certifies you are unable to use the facility. Attach the provider note.
  • Your contract term ended. Once the initial term is over and the membership runs month to month, written notice ends the auto-renewal instead of another year of dues.
  • You already cancelled and they kept charging. A second written notice with EFT revocation, sent certified, creates the paper trail your bank needs to block the debits.
  • The gym cut services or closed. A material reduction in hours, equipment, or amenities, or a location closure, is a breach ground in many contracts.

Check Your Contract Before You Mail

Four details in your membership agreement decide how the letter should read, so check them before you generate it:

  • Notice period. Many contracts require 30 days written notice. Set the effective date in the letter to match it, or the gym can bill one more cycle.
  • Early termination fee. Fixed-term contracts often charge one. Knowing the amount up front keeps it from looking like a billing error later.
  • Where to send it. The contract names a cancellation address, often a corporate office or PO box, not the front desk. Mailing to the wrong address is the most common reason a cancellation "never arrived."
  • Auto-renewal date. Note the date the term renews and mail the letter at least a full notice period before it.

After You Mail It: Make the Charges Stop

The letter is step one. These steps close the loop:

  1. Keep the green card. When the Return Receipt comes back, staple it to your copy of the letter. That date is your proof of delivery.
  2. Tell your bank in writing. Send your bank a copy of the letter and the delivery proof and revoke the preauthorized debits under Federal Regulation E (12 CFR § 1005.10(c)).
  3. Watch two statement cycles. A charge that lands after your effective date is a billing error. Dispute it with the bank, not just the gym.
  4. Escalate if billing continues. File a complaint with your state attorney general or consumer protection office, attaching the letter and the green card.

This page is general information, not legal advice. If the gym sues over a balance or sends it to collections, talk to a local consumer attorney.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why won't gyms allow easy online cancellations?

Many health clubs rely on friction-based retention: no online cancel button, a phone maze, a form that only exists at the front desk. The FTC's 2024 “click to cancel” rule never took effect: the Eighth Circuit vacated it on July 8, 2025, before its compliance date. What still applies is the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act, state auto-renewal laws, and your state health club statute, so a formal certified letter remains the most reliable way to end a membership with proof.

Why do gyms require written cancellation via certified mail?

Many health clubs (such as Planet Fitness or LA Fitness) intentionally make online or phone cancellations difficult. State Health Club Services Acts generally recognize written notice via USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt Requested as a legally binding notification date, providing proof of delivery that prevents gyms from continuing to charge dues.

Can a gym continue charging my bank account after I cancel?

No. Under Federal Regulation E (12 CFR § 1005.10(c)), consumers have the legal right to stop payment and revoke recurring electronic fund transfers (EFT/ACH) by notifying both the merchant and their financial institution. This letter includes explicit language revoking all future billing authorizations.

What is Federal Regulation E and how does it protect me?

Under Federal Regulation E (12 CFR § 1005.10(c)), you have the statutory right to stop preauthorized electronic fund transfers (ACH debits) at any time. By sending written revocation to the gym and submitting proof to your bank, your financial institution is legally obligated to block any unauthorized debits.

How does the 25-mile relocation rule work?

Most state Health Club Services Acts require fitness clubs to allow cancellation if a member permanently moves more than 25 miles away from the home facility without an affiliated facility nearby. Attaching a utility bill, new lease, or driver's license showing your new address satisfies this statutory requirement.

What should I send with my cancellation letter?

Send your signed cancellation letter, your key tag or membership barcode copy, and any supporting documentation (e.g. proof of relocation like a utility bill, or a physician note if canceling for medical reasons) via USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt Requested (PS Form 3811).

How long does it take for a gym cancellation to take effect?

It depends on your contract. Many memberships require 30 days' written notice, so the cancellation takes effect at the end of the notice period, not the day you mail the letter. Set the effective date in the letter to match your contract, keep the certified mail receipt, and watch your statements: any charge after the effective date can be disputed with your bank.

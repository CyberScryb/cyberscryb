Your Name Is Your First Marketing

People decide whether to click, walk in, or remember you based on your name long before they see your product. A great business name does three jobs at once: it sticks in memory, it hints at what you do or how you feel, and it survives being said out loud, typed into a search bar, and printed on a sign.

The trap most founders fall into is brainstorming in a vacuum. You come up with twelve names, half are taken, three are unpronounceable, and the rest sound like everyone else in your industry. Naming is a volume game — you need dozens of candidates before the right one surfaces, and that is exactly what a generator is for.

Our AI Business Name Generator uses Google’s Gemini model to produce name ideas tuned to your industry and the vibe you want — modern, playful, professional, bold, minimalist, or luxurious. Use the output as a shortlist, then run your favorites through the real checks: domain availability, trademark search, and the say-it-out-loud test. Our companion guide walks through all three.

How It Works

Step 1: Describe the business. A sentence or two about what you sell and who it is for. Keywords help — "coffee," "Portland," "artisan" all steer the results.

Step 2: Set industry and vibe. The naming conventions for a law firm and a taco truck are not the same. Tell the generator which world you live in.

Step 3: Generate and shortlist. Get 3 to 5 distinct ideas. Star the ones that make you feel something — naming is emotional before it is logical.

Step 4: Validate the winners. Check domain availability, search the USPTO trademark database, and say each name out loud five times. Kill anything awkward.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a good business name?

Short, easy to spell, easy to say, and emotionally on-brand. The best names also pass the "radio test" — if someone hears it once, they can spell it. Avoid clever spellings that cost you customers at every mention.

How do I check if a business name is taken?

Three checks: search the USPTO trademark database for federal conflicts, check your state’s business registry, and see if the .com domain (or a clean alternative) is available. Our guide walks through each step.

Should my business name include keywords?

It can help with clarity ("Portland Dog Grooming") but hurts distinctiveness. Brandable invented names ("Roverly") are harder to start and easier to own. Most successful brands pick one lane.

How many name ideas should I generate?

Dozens. Naming is a numbers game — generate freely, shortlist ruthlessly, and validate only your top three. This tool gives you 3 or 5 per round, so run it a few times with different vibes.

Is this business name generator free?

Yes. 100% free with unlimited generations, no accounts, and no signup required.

Can I trademark a name from this generator?

Possibly, but trademark law is fact-specific. AI-generated names have no special status — what matters is whether the name is distinctive and not confusingly similar to an existing mark in your industry. Talk to a trademark attorney before investing heavily.

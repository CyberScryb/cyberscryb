What actually matters

Put the request in writing

Verbal leave requests get misremembered. A written letter states the reason, the start date, and the expected return date in one document HR can file. Date it, keep a copy, and note how you delivered it. The letter is your record that notice was given.

State the schedule plainly

Continuous leave is one block of time. Intermittent leave is separate blocks or a reduced schedule, such as two days a week for treatment. If you need intermittent leave, describe the pattern exactly: the days, the hours, and what drives the schedule. Vague wording invites disputes later.

Answer the self-check honestly

The three questions above (time employed, hours worked, employer size) are the basics people run into with FMLA. Your answers go into the draft as your own statements, so HR has the context up front. This tool never decides whether you qualify; only HR can confirm that.

Certification is separate from the letter

Employers often ask for medical certification from a health care provider to support the leave. The letter is the request; the certification is the proof. Mention in the letter that you can provide certification if HR requires it, and get the paperwork from your provider promptly.

FAQ

How do I write an FMLA request letter?

State that you are requesting leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act, give the reason in plain terms, state the start date and expected return date, say whether the leave is continuous or intermittent, and note how you can be reached during leave. This tool drafts that letter from your facts. It is general information, not legal advice.

Do I qualify for FMLA leave?

This tool does not assess eligibility. The usual questions are whether you have worked for the employer for 12 months, worked 1,250 hours in the prior 12 months, and whether the employer has 50 employees within 75 miles of your worksite. Only your HR department can confirm eligibility for your situation. The self-check above collects your answers for the draft; it is not a finding.

Can I take FMLA leave intermittently?

Intermittent leave means taking leave in separate blocks or working a reduced schedule instead of one continuous block. Your letter should describe the exact schedule you need, based on what your health care provider supports. Use the intermittent mode above to draft that version of the letter.

How much notice do I need to give for FMLA leave?

When the need is foreseeable, employees are generally expected to give 30 days' notice; when it is not foreseeable, notice is expected as soon as practicable. Check your employer's policy and HR for the exact process. A written request with a date on it protects you either way.

What does my employer need from my doctor?

Employers commonly require medical certification from a health care provider to support FMLA leave. The letter is the request; the certification is the proof. Say in the letter that you can provide certification if HR requires it, and get the paperwork from your provider promptly. HR will usually supply the form they want used.

Should I talk to an employment lawyer about my leave?

If your leave was denied, your job was affected while you were out, or you were treated differently after requesting leave, talking to an employment attorney early is worth the consultation. This tool drafts the request letter from your facts; it does not assess your rights and it is not a substitute for counsel.

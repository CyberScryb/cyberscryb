How lemon law demands actually work

The letter goes to the manufacturer

The dealer tried to fix the car; the manufacturer owes the remedy. A demand letter sent to the dealer alone does not start a lemon law claim. Find the manufacturer's claims address in the warranty booklet or owner's manual.

The repair orders are the claim

A demand letter without dated repair orders is a complaint. With them, it is a claim. Every visit needs a dated repair order showing the complaint, what was done, and how many days the vehicle was out of service. Get copies of all of them before you write.

The presumption is a starting point, not a verdict

Most states presume a lemon after about 4 attempts or 30 days out of service, but the exact numbers, the time window, and the mileage cap are state-specific, and a safety defect can change the count. The claim panel shows your numbers against the common presumption; your state's law decides.

One specific demand, one deadline

Ask for a repurchase, a replacement, or a refund, and give a date to respond by. Letters that ask the manufacturer to "make it right" get stalled. A specific demand with a deadline forces a specific answer.

Certified mail creates the paper trail

The return receipt proves the manufacturer got the letter and when. An email can be ignored or lost. A certified letter has a signature, and the follow-up round depends on being able to cite the first mailing by date and tracking number.

Do not accept the first offer without advice

Manufacturers often offer a trade assist, an extended warranty, or a partial payment before they offer a repurchase. Those offers can waive your claim. Talk to a lemon law attorney in your state before you sign or accept anything.

Common questions

What is a lemon law demand letter?
A formal written claim sent to the vehicle's manufacturer stating that the vehicle has a defect the dealer could not fix after a reasonable number of attempts while it was under warranty, and demanding a specific remedy: a repurchase, a replacement vehicle, or a refund. The dealer tried to fix the car; the manufacturer owes the remedy, which is why the letter goes to the manufacturer.
How many repair attempts does a lemon law claim need?
Most states use a presumption around three or four repair attempts for the same defect, or about 30 days out of service, inside the first year or two of ownership. The exact numbers, the time window, and the mileage cap vary by state, so confirm your own state's thresholds before you send anything. This tool shows your numbers next to the common presumption so you can see where you stand.
Who do I send a lemon law demand letter to?
The manufacturer, at the address in your warranty booklet or owner's manual, usually by certified mail with return receipt requested. The dealer is not the party that owes a repurchase or replacement. Keep the certified receipt; it starts the paper trail and proves the manufacturer got the letter.
What should a lemon law demand letter include?
Your name, the vehicle year, make, model, and VIN, the purchase date and price, the defect in plain words, every repair attempt with dates and the dealer that did the work, the total days the vehicle was out of service, the mileage at the first repair, and one specific demand: repurchase, replacement, or refund, with a deadline to respond.
Does the manufacturer get one last chance to fix it?
Many states require it. The manufacturer gets a final repair opportunity after you notify them in writing, often within a set number of days. The final-repair-opportunity mode of this tool drafts that notice instead of a full demand, so you do not skip a step your state may require.
What if the manufacturer ignores the demand letter?
Follow up in writing after about 21 days, referencing the first mailing date and tracking number. If there is still no answer after about 45 days, talk to a lemon law attorney in your state before you accept any offer or file anything. Many lemon law attorneys work on contingency, paid by the manufacturer if you win.
Does the lemon law cover used cars?
Usually not under the new-car lemon law, but some states have a separate used-car lemon law with a shorter window, and the federal Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act can still apply when a warranty was breached. Tell the tool the vehicle was used and give the warranty terms; the letter will state the facts as they are.
What is the repair log and why does it matter?
The repair log is your dated list of every repair attempt: when the vehicle went in, which dealer, what the complaint was, and how many days it was out of service. It is the evidence the whole claim rests on. Without dated repair orders, a demand letter is just a complaint. Build the log from your repair orders before you generate the letter.

Spot the Scam Call

Fourteen fictional phone calls, written out. Some are scams. Some are ordinary. The caller ID may be lying. Hang up, keep talking, or pick a reply, then read the red flags. There is no audio, and nothing is dialed.

  2. Name the ask A code, a gift card, crypto, or a secret is the script, not the voice.
  3. Call back yourself Use the number on the card, the bill, or the one already saved.

How to take a call you did not expect

The name on the screen is a costume. Caller ID is a label the calling side can set, so a phone can say Northwind, a clinic, or a grandchild and still be someone else. In this exercise the companies are fictional and the numbers use the 555-01 exchange, which is reserved for fiction. Nothing here can ring a real phone.

The useful habit is the same on a real handset. If you did not start the call, you do not owe it your time. A real office can leave a message, and you can call back on a number you already had: the one printed on the card, the bill, the appointment card, or the bottle. A number spoken by the caller was chosen by the caller.

A hurry, a code, a gift card

“Locks in ten minutes” and “shuts off in thirty minutes” are there to skip the question of who is talking. A code from a text proves that a login or a payment can be finished. Reading it aloud hands that proof over. Gift cards and a crypto transfer are cash that cannot be pulled back. A caller who adds “do not tell anyone” or “do not call the office” is asking you to skip the one check that would end the script.

A tax-office call is the shape people meet when someone pretends to be a revenue agency, including the version aimed at the IRS. A real agency does not collect a bill in gift cards, and it does not threaten arrest on a call you did not start. A device company does not phone because a computer “reported a virus,” and a parcel company does not take a redelivery fee in gift cards. A warranty does not expire because a stranger wants a card number before you hang up.

AI voice clones and a family safe word

A cloned voice can sound like a grandchild, a child, or a partner. The voice is not proof, and a familiar name on the screen is not proof either. The script around it is older than the software: an emergency, a short clock, gift cards or crypto, and an order to keep it secret.

Agree on a family safe word in person, before anyone needs it. If a call sounds like family and asks for money, ask for the word. Then hang up and call that person back on the number already saved in the phone. Do not use the number that just called. A real emergency still gets help when you reach them yourself, or when you call a local number you already know. The caller who refuses that step is the problem.

FAQ

Does this upload my answers?

No. The quiz runs in your browser. The only thing saved is your best score, in this browser's local storage. Individual answers are not stored and are not sent to analytics. You can confirm that in the network panel: answering a call adds no request.

Does this play audio or place a call?

No. The calls are written transcripts. There is no audio, no microphone, and no phone call. The number on the screen is text. Nothing is dialed.

Are the companies or people real?

No. Northwind, Harborline, Rivermark, Vela, Lumenwatt, Pine Clinic, Cedar Staffing, and Example Pharmacy are fictional. The only person named is Alex Example, a placeholder. Phone numbers use the 555-01 exchange reserved for fiction.

What should I do when a voice sounds like family?

A cloned voice can sound like someone you love. Ask for a safe word you agreed on in person, then hang up and call them back on the number already saved in your phone. Do not use the number that called. Do not buy gift cards, send crypto, or keep it a secret.

Does a perfect score mean I will catch a real scam call?

No. This is practice on a fixed set of scripts. A real caller can lie without these patterns, and a real office can leave a reminder. When money, a code, or a familiar voice is involved, hang up and use a number you already had.

Where does the best score live, and can I play again?

Play again shuffles the same fictional calls. Your best score stays in this browser only. Clearing site data for cyberscryb.com removes it. The round itself is forgotten when you leave or shuffle.

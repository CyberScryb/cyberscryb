A Useful Request Gives Someone a Decision to Make

Name the purchase, describe the problem, and ask for the outcome you want. "The lamp arrived with a cracked base" gives a support person something to investigate. "This is unacceptable" leaves them looking for the actual problem.

Use the optional fields for an order number, amount, previous contact, and records. The draft only includes details you provide. It does not invent a policy, claim a legal right, or promise that the seller will agree.

How It Works

Step 1: Add the required facts. Company, item or service, what went wrong, and what you are asking for. Those four fields are all the tool needs to build a coherent draft.

Step 2: Add the evidence you have. Order or reference number, amount paid, purchase date, prior contact, and the records behind the claim. Optional fields are where weak requests get strong.

Step 3: Edit the draft like the recipient. Read it as the person who decides. Cut anything they cannot verify, and make sure the requested outcome is explicit.

Step 4: Send it through the real channel. Copy, download, or print the text, then submit it where the seller actually handles returns: their support email, help center, or dispute form.

Real Ways People Use It

Online orders: Damaged, wrong, or missing items from a web store, with the order number and delivery date attached.

Services that fell short: A no-show contractor, a repair that failed, a service described one way and delivered another.

Travel and events: Canceled bookings and experiences that did not match the listing, sent to the operator's claims process before the payment provider's deadline.

Charges and warranties: Subscriptions that renewed unexpectedly, duplicate charges, and warranty claims the first denial letter tried to end.

Before You Send It

  1. Check the seller's current return or complaints process. An email may not start a return in their system.
  2. Read every date, amount, and reference in the draft. Add the specific refund amount if it differs from what you paid.
  3. Attach relevant evidence yourself, through the appropriate channel. Keep originals and avoid sending unrelated personal information.
  4. Save the message you actually send and any confirmation. A draft is not a submission receipt.

A requested reply date is your request. It does not extend a marketplace, payment-provider, or legal deadline. Check those separately if they apply.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this refund request letter builder free?

Yes. It is free with no account and no signup. The draft is built in your browser and you can copy, download, or print it.

What should I include in a refund request?

Name the seller, the item or service, exactly what went wrong, and the outcome you want (refund, replacement, or repair). Add the order number, amount paid, and purchase date when you have them, and mention any previous contact with dates or case numbers.

Does this tool send the request for me?

No. This tool creates an editable draft only. You review it, then send it yourself through the seller's actual channel: support email, help center, or dispute form. An email may not start a return in their system, so check their returns process first.

Is what I enter kept private?

Yes. Your details stay in the page. They are not sent to AI, stored by this tool, or used to contact anyone on your behalf.

What if the seller refuses the refund?

Escalate calmly: open a dispute with the marketplace or payment provider, request a chargeback through your card issuer, or file a complaint with consumer protection in your state or country. Check each route's deadline, because they are often short.

Can I ask for a replacement or repair instead of a refund?

Yes. The tool lets you pick refund, replacement, or repair as the requested outcome. Sometimes a replacement or a repair is more likely to succeed, for example when the problem is a defect covered by warranty.

What actually matters

The 15-year line decides the form

The Discharge Review Board hears DD-293 applications for separations less than 15 years ago. Past 15 years, or for a discharge from a general court-martial, the case goes to the Board for Correction of Military Records on a DD-149. Filing the wrong form wastes months. The form check above works from the separation date you enter, so copy it straight off your DD-214.

Grounds, not grievances

Boards decide on inequity, impropriety, or clemency. Inequity means the discharge was unfair in the circumstances. Impropriety means an error was made or a rule was not followed. Clemency means what has changed since service warrants a second look. A packet that names its grounds and proves each one with records beats a packet that just says the discharge was unfair.

Records beat memory

“I was a good soldier” persuades no one. Evaluations, awards, counseling statements, treatment records, and the separation paperwork are what the board reads. Buddy statements from people who served with you fill the gaps the record leaves. Gather every record before you file, and send new evidence the way the board requires, not by guessing.

Free help exists, use it

Accredited VSO representatives from the American Legion, VFW, DAV, and your state veterans agency help veterans file these applications for free, and they have filed hundreds of them. They know what each board wants to see and will review your packet before you send it. Bring them a finished draft, not a blank form, and the review goes faster.

FAQ

What is a military discharge upgrade?

A discharge upgrade is a change to the characterization of your discharge on your DD-214, for example from other than honorable to general under honorable conditions. It is decided by a Discharge Review Board on a DD-293 application, or by a Board for Correction of Military Records on a DD-149 application. The board looks at whether the discharge was inequitable or improper, or whether clemency is warranted, based on the records and the evidence you submit. This is general information, not legal advice.

DD-293 vs DD-149: which form do I use for a discharge upgrade?

Use DD-293 if you separated less than 15 years ago: it goes to your service's Discharge Review Board. Use DD-149 if more than 15 years have passed since your separation, or if your discharge came from a general court-martial: it goes to your service's Board for Correction of Military Records. The 15-year line runs from your separation date, and this tool computes it from the date you enter. Confirm the right form with the board or an accredited veterans service organization before you file.

Can I upgrade a dishonorable discharge?

A dishonorable discharge from a general court-martial cannot be reviewed by a Discharge Review Board, so a DD-293 is not the route. Those cases go to the Board for Correction of Military Records on a DD-149. Other characterizations, including other than honorable and bad conduct from a special court-martial, can be reviewed by a Discharge Review Board within 15 years of separation. Whether any discharge can be upgraded depends on the facts and the record, and no tool can predict the outcome. An accredited veterans service organization or an attorney can review your DD-214 and tell you which route fits.

How long does a discharge upgrade take?

Discharge review cases commonly take many months, and a year or more is not unusual, because the board has to pull your full service record and work through a large caseload. Some boards offer a records review on the papers only, and some offer a personal appearance hearing, which can add time. File as soon as your packet is ready rather than waiting for a perfect packet, and keep copies of everything you send. This is general information, not legal advice.

What evidence helps a discharge upgrade application?

The strongest evidence is paper that shows what happened and who you are now: your DD-214, service treatment records, performance evaluations, awards, counseling statements, the separation paperwork, and any investigation or court-martial records. For clemency, add proof of what changed since service: steady employment, education, treatment records, sobriety or counseling records, community service, and character letters from employers, family, or community members. Buddy statements from people who served with you can fill gaps the record leaves. The packet mode turns your evidence list into a checklist.

Does a discharge upgrade restore VA benefits?

An upgrade can help, but it does not automatically restore benefits. The VA makes its own character-of-discharge determination for benefit eligibility, separate from the military review boards, and some upgraded characterizations still need a VA decision. An other than honorable discharge blocks most VA benefits while it stands, which is why an upgrade matters, but treat the VA question as its own step: ask the VA or an accredited representative how your new characterization affects the specific benefit you need. This is general information, not legal advice.

Do I need a lawyer for a discharge upgrade?

No. Many veterans file with help from an accredited veterans service organization representative, such as the American Legion, VFW, DAV, or your state veterans agency, and that help is free. A representative who has filed many of these cases knows what each board wants to see and can review your packet before you send it. An attorney is worth considering when the case is complicated, such as a court-martial discharge or a case that was already denied once. This tool builds a draft packet for that review; it is not a lawyer.

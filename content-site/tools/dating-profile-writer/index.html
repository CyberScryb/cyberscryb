Your Profile Is a First Impression, Not a Resume

On dating apps, your profile gets about three seconds before someone swipes. Most profiles fail the same way: a list of adjectives ("fun, adventurous, loyal"), a height, and a fish photo. None of that gives anyone a reason to start a conversation — and conversation is the entire point.

What works is specificity. "Weekend hiker" is forgettable; "currently training for the Wonderland Trail and accepting snack recommendations" is a conversation starter. The best profiles sound like a friend describing you at your best: warm, specific, and just self-aware enough to be charming.

Our AI Dating Profile Writer uses Google’s Gemini model to turn your raw details into polished bios tuned to your app — punchy for Tinder, prompt-friendly for Hinge, confident for Bumble — and the tone you want. Pick the version that sounds most like you, tweak the details, and pair it with good photos using the tips in our companion guide.

How It Works

Step 1: Describe yourself honestly. A few sentences: what you do, what you love, what you are looking for. Specifics beat adjectives every time.

Step 2: Pick your app and tone. Hinge rewards prompt-style personality; Tinder rewards brevity and wit. Match the tool to the room.

Step 3: Generate and compare. Get 3 to 5 bios with different angles. Read them out loud — the one that makes you smile is the one.

Step 4: Make it yours. Swap in your real details, cut anything that does not sound like you, and update your photos to match the energy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a good dating profile?

Specificity, warmth, and one conversation hook. Name real hobbies, show a little personality, and end with something easy to reply to ("Tell me your most controversial food opinion"). Avoid adjective lists and negativity about exes or what you "don’t want."

How long should my dating profile be?

Short enough to read in seconds: 40 to 80 words for Tinder and Bumble, slightly longer for Hinge where prompts carry the weight. If it takes scrolling, it is too long.

Should I use humor in my profile?

If it is genuinely you, yes — wit is the highest-converting tone in profile studies. But forced jokes read worse than sincerity. Pick the tone setting that matches your actual personality.

What photos should I pair with my bio?

Clear face photo first, full-body second, then you doing things you love. One group photo max. Profiles with 4-6 varied photos get significantly more matches than one-selfie profiles.

Goodbyes Are Networking in Disguise

Most farewell messages read like they were written by a greeting card company: "Wishing you all the best in your future endeavors!" Nobody remembers those. The ones people keep are the ones with a specific memory in them, because a specific memory proves the relationship was real. "I will never forget the night we stayed until 2am to save the launch" does more work than ten lines of generic well-wishing.

The formula is simple: one concrete detail, one genuine thanks tied to something they actually did, and one forward-looking line with a real way to stay in touch. That is it. The tool builds your message around the memory you give it, so it sounds like you on a good day instead of a template on autopilot.

Then make it yours before you send it. Swap in the nickname, the inside joke, the real detail only you two share. One true line is the difference between a message they screenshot and a message they skim.

How It Works

Step 1: Say who the message is for. A coworker who is leaving gets a different message than your whole team when you are the one walking out. Pick the direction so the voice lands right.

Step 2: Set the tone. Warm for people you will miss, professional for bosses and clients, funny for the work friends who will roast you for being sentimental.

Step 3: Give it one real memory. This is the whole trick. One specific detail — a project, a joke, a habit — turns a generic goodbye into one they remember.

Step 4: Send it before the last day. A farewell that arrives on time is a goodbye. A farewell that arrives a week late is an apology wearing a costume.

Real Ways People Use It

A coworker is leaving for a new job: The goodbye card or Slack message that goes beyond "good luck!" — one shared memory, real thanks, and a standing invitation to stay in touch.

Your own last day: The team-wide email or message when you are the one leaving. Grateful, specific, forward-looking — the message that keeps the door open for references, referrals, and future roles.

Retirement goodbyes: Longer, warmer messages for someone closing out a career. The tool keeps it dignified and specific instead of drifting into greeting-card territory.

Remote team farewells: When you never shared an office, the goodbye has to work harder. A message that names what remote collaboration actually looked like — the async wins, the timezone heroics — lands with people you only knew through a screen.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do you write in a farewell message to a coworker?

One specific memory, genuine thanks for something concrete, and a forward-looking wish with a way to stay in touch. Specificity is the whole game.

How long should a farewell message be?

50 to 100 words for a card or Slack message; 150 to 250 for an email to a team or manager. Longer than that and it is a speech, not a message.

Should a farewell message be funny or serious?

Match the relationship. Funny works for close work friends; warm and professional for bosses, clients, and company-wide goodbyes. One light line inside a sincere message is the safe play.

What should you NOT say in a farewell message?

No negativity about the company, no gossip about the departure, no backhanded compliments, and no promises to stay in touch that you will not keep.

Is it better to send a farewell by email or Slack?

Slack or Teams for close coworkers, email for team-wide goodbyes and messages to managers or clients. Send it before the last day either way.

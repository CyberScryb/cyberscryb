Term Decoder: Plain-English Cards

Every term this tool recognizes, decoded. Click a highlighted term in your pasted text above to jump straight to its card.

Deductible

What you pay out of pocket each year before the plan starts paying its share for most services. Copays and preventive care often skip the deductible. A $3,500 deductible means the first $3,500 of covered bills are yours.

Out-of-Pocket Maximum

The most you pay in a year for covered, in-network care: deductible + copays + coinsurance, added up. After you hit it, the plan pays 100% of covered costs for the rest of the year. Premiums do not count toward it.

Coinsurance

Your percentage share of a bill after the deductible. 20% coinsurance on a $1,000 covered bill means you pay $200 and the plan pays $800.

Copay / Copayment

A flat fee you pay at the visit or pharmacy, like $30 for a primary-care visit. Usually counts toward the out-of-pocket maximum, usually does not count toward the deductible.

Premium

The monthly bill that keeps your coverage active, whether you use care or not. Annual cost = monthly premium × 12. Premiums count toward neither the deductible nor the out-of-pocket maximum.

In-Network vs Out-of-Network

In-network providers have a contract with your plan: lower negotiated prices, and your spending counts toward the deductible and out-of-pocket max. Out-of-network care costs more and may not count at all; some plans cover almost none of it.

Prior Authorization

Your plan's advance permission for certain services, drugs, or procedures. Without it the claim can be denied even when the care itself is covered in theory. Your doctor's office usually files it.

Formulary

The plan's list of covered drugs, split into tiers. Generic tier = cheapest copay; brand and specialty tiers cost more. A drug not on the formulary may cost you full price.

HSA / FSA

HSA (Health Savings Account): a tax-advantaged account paired with high-deductible plans; the money rolls over and is yours. FSA (Flexible Spending Account): a similar tax break through an employer, but funds usually expire each year ("use it or lose it"). Both can pay deductibles, copays, and coinsurance.

Preventive Care

Screenings, vaccines, and annual checkups that most plans must cover at $0 before the deductible: no copay, no coinsurance, as long as the visit is in-network and coded as preventive.

How It Works

Step 1: Enter the plan basics. Plan name, monthly premium, annual deductible, annual out-of-pocket maximum, and the coinsurance percentage you pay after the deductible. These come straight from the plan's summary page.

Step 2: Enter the common copays. What a primary-care visit, specialist visit, ER visit, urgent care visit, and generic or brand prescription cost you out of pocket. Unknowns can stay at 0.

Step 3: Decode the jargon. Paste the dense paragraph from your Summary of Benefits. Known terms get highlighted; click any highlighted term to jump to its plain-English card below. Set a sample bill amount and hit "Explain My Plan".

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this health insurance plan explainer giving me insurance advice?

No. This tool is educational only. It decodes insurance terms into plain English and does the arithmetic on the numbers you enter. It does not recommend a plan and is not insurance advice. For enrollment decisions, check healthcare.gov or talk to a licensed broker.

Is the information I enter into the tool private?

Yes. This tool runs 100% in your browser with zero network requests: no fetch calls, no AI service, no tracking of your inputs. Nothing you type leaves your device. Saved plans live in your browser's localStorage, on your device only.

What is the difference between a deductible and an out-of-pocket maximum?

The deductible is what you pay first each year before the plan starts sharing costs. The out-of-pocket maximum caps everything you pay in cost-sharing for covered in-network care in a year: deductible plus copays plus coinsurance. After you hit it, the plan pays 100% of covered costs for the rest of the year. Premiums count toward neither.

Do copays count toward the deductible?

On most ACA marketplace plans, no: copays count toward the out-of-pocket maximum but do not satisfy the deductible. Some plans work differently, so your plan's Summary of Benefits and Coverage is the final word. This tool prints its assumptions next to the math so you can check them against your plan.

How are the quiet, typical, and rough year totals computed?

Deterministic arithmetic, shown line by line. A quiet year is annual premiums plus two PCP copays. A typical year is premiums plus six PCP visits, three specialist visits, and twelve generic prescriptions, with cost-sharing capped at the out-of-pocket maximum. A rough year assumes a $25,000 surgery bill: the deductible applies first, then your coinsurance percentage on the remainder, plus the ER copay, capped at the out-of-pocket maximum.

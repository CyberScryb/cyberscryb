What actually works

Why specifics win

Utility CSRs and hardship desks process volume. Letters that open with account #, disconnect date, and a dollar offer get acted on. Emotional-only letters get filed.

LIHEAP & local aid

Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) and state crisis funds can pay vendors directly. Apply the same day you call — funding windows close. Search energyhelp.us or your state human services site.

Medical certificates

Rules vary by state and utility. Usually a licensed clinician must certify that disconnection would be especially dangerous. The utility provides the form; the letter should request that process, not invent a diagnosis.

Get it in writing

Verbal “we’ll put a hold” evaporates. Ask for email confirmation, a case/reference number, and the exact date any hold or plan expires.

Real ways people use a utility shutoff letter

Deferred payment agreement after job loss

A layoff or cut in shift hours leaves a $600+ electric balance past due. Offering a specific down payment this week plus a fixed monthly installment on top of current bills is the standard structure utility collections desks approve.

30-day medical hold for oxygen or serious illness

When a household member relies on a nebulizer, CPAP, home dialysis, or post-surgery recovery, a written request paired with a licensed physician’s medical certificate triggers a mandatory state-regulated hold while payment terms are arranged.

LIHEAP crisis pledge pending

A community action agency approves a Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program crisis grant, but the check takes weeks to reach the utility. Sending the agency reference number and requesting an administrative hold prevents disconnection in the gap.

Reconnection after same-day shutoff

If power or gas was already disconnected, calling Restoration with a verifiable partial payment receipt, household vulnerability details, and a written payment plan speeds up next-business-day reconnection.

FAQ

Will this letter stop a utility shutoff by itself?

No. The letter documents your request in writing, but you should also call the utility's payment arrangements or credit desk before the disconnect date, confirm any hold or deferred payment agreement, and submit required medical certification forms.

What should I say when I call the utility company?

Give your account number first, then say: “I received a disconnect notice for [date]. I can pay $X by [day] and $Y per month on top of current bills. Please place my account on a deferred payment arrangement or hardship hold and email me the confirmation number.” Follow up with your written letter.

How does a medical certificate or medical baseline hold work?

If a resident has a serious illness or relies on electrically powered medical equipment (such as oxygen concentrators, CPAP, nebulizers, or home dialysis), state utility regulations typically allow a licensed physician, nurse practitioner, or physician assistant to submit a medical certification that pauses disconnection for 30 to 90 days while you arrange payment.

What are winter and extreme-heat PUC shutoff moratoriums?

Most state Public Utility Commissions (PUCs) enforce seasonal shutoff moratoriums that bar utilities from disconnecting heating service during winter months (often November through March) or cooling service during extreme heat advisories for income-eligible, elderly, or medically vulnerable households.

Is LIHEAP only for winter heating bills?

No. Many states run year-round LIHEAP crisis assistance and summer cooling programs. When a community action agency issues a crisis pledge on your account, the utility typically places a hold on disconnection while the grant payment processes.

What if my power or water has already been disconnected?

Switch the tool to Restore after shutoff mode. Call the utility's Restoration department with a concrete immediate payment amount, ask whether a LIHEAP crisis pledge or medical certification can reduce the reconnection down payment, and request same-day or next-business-day restoration.

Is this legal advice?

No. Utility tariffs and consumer protections are governed by your state Public Utility Commission and local provider rules. Use this letter as a structured draft and contact legal aid or your state PUC consumer division if a utility violates shutoff protections.

        Where the quiet money goes

        A subscription leak is a charge that keeps clearing after the habit that justified it has faded. One streaming service is a choice. Four of them, plus a music app you opened last spring and a delivery membership on a second phone, is a pile of small drafts that never show up as a single bill. This page adds those drafts up in front of you.

        Check only what you pay for. The starting price is an estimate for one common US plan, and the row says which plan that estimate refers to. If your statement says something else, type that number. A promo, a student plan, an annual prepay, or a family seat will not match the default, and the yearly total is only as honest as the prices you leave in place.

        Then mark how often you actually open each one: often, sometimes, or rarely. The overlap list is about the same job, not about owning both a game library and a newspaper. Three video services is a flag. A game pass and a music app is not. If a plan already includes another one on your list, such as YouTube Premium and YouTube Music, or Creative Cloud and a separate Photoshop seat, the included one is called out even if you use it. A Disney bundle note shows up when Disney+ and Hulu are both selected, because the bundle can cost less than dropping one at random.

        The cut list is a suggestion, not an order. Rarely used services come off first. In a crowded group, a service you only use sometimes is suggested when another one in that group is the one you use often. Anything marked often stays, unless you are paying a second time for something already included. If a suggestion is wrong, change the use. The savings line updates with it.

        Nothing here cancels a plan, and nothing is sent to a server. The optional save stays in this browser under a label you can clear. Before you drop a service, look for an annual commitment or a cancellation fee, and put the money you free somewhere on purpose. The budget planner is the place to seat that surplus. If it should attack a balance, the debt payoff calculator shows what the extra payment does. If a gym membership is the line you are done with, the gym cancellation letter is the note to send.

        FAQ

        Does this send my subscription list to a server?

        No. The math runs in your browser. If you leave the list in place, it is saved in this browser's local storage so you can come back to it. That copy never leaves the device. Clear saved list deletes it. You can confirm there is no upload in the browser network panel.

        Are the starting prices exact?

        No. Each default is a typical US estimate for the plan named on that row, not a quote from the company. Promos, annual billing, student plans, bundles, and taxes change the real charge. Edit every price to match your bill before you treat the total as yours.

        What counts as an overlap?

        An overlap is two or more selected services that do the same job, such as several video streaming services, two music apps, or two meal-delivery memberships. A game subscription and a music app are not treated as overlap. Three or more in one group is flagged. A separate note appears when one plan already includes another, such as YouTube Premium and YouTube Music, or when a bundle may cost less than the separate plans.

        How does the calculator decide what to cut?

        It suggests a cut when you mark a service as rarely used, when you are paying for a plan that another selected plan already includes, or when three or more services in the same group are selected and this one is only used sometimes while another is used often. Anything you mark as used often stays, unless you are paying twice for a plan that is already included. The suggestions are optional. Change the use if a suggestion is wrong.

        Is it free, and does it cancel anything for me?

        It is free, with no account and no signup. It does not cancel, pause, or contact any company. You cancel with each service yourself. Some plans bill annually, charge a cancellation fee, or are cheaper in a bundle, so check the real terms before you drop one.

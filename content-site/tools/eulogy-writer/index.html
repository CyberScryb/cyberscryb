Nobody Is Ready to Give This Speech

Being asked to deliver a eulogy is an honor wrapped in a dread. You want to do right by the person, you are grieving, and you have to stand in front of a room and speak. The fear is not really about writing — it is about standing up with nothing to say and everyone watching.

This tool gives you the bones: an opening that introduces you, one or two of your real stories shaped for speaking, the turn toward what they meant, and a closing that lands. It is paced for reading aloud, with natural pauses and sentences that sound like speech, not print. Short sentences. Room to breathe.

Then rehearse it twice, out loud, standing up. Mark where you will pause. It is fine to cry a little — the room expects it. What they will remember is that you showed up and spoke the truth about someone you loved.

How It Works

Step 1: Name them and your relationship. "My father," "her college roommate" — the room needs to know who is speaking.

Step 2: Pour in real memories. Specific beats general every time. The fishing trip, the advice, the laugh, the thing they always said. Fragments are fine.

Step 3: Pick tone and length. Three minutes is the sweet spot for most services. Five if the memories warrant it.

Step 4: Make it yours and rehearse. Edit in your voice, read it aloud twice, and bring the printed page — nobody expects you to memorize this.

Real Ways People Use It

Funeral services: A 3-minute eulogy from your memories, structured for the chapel or graveside.

Celebrations of life: A warmer, story-driven version for the less formal gathering.

Pet memorials: The same structure works for the animals that were family too.

Shared family writing: One person drafts, the family each adds a memory, and the final version carries everyone's voice.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a eulogy be?

Three to five minutes read aloud, roughly 400 to 700 words. Shorter and specific beats longer and general.

What is the structure of a good eulogy?

Opening (who you are), one or two specific stories, the turn toward what they meant, and a short closing farewell.

What is the difference between a eulogy and an obituary?

A eulogy is a speech, meant to be heard. An obituary is a published notice, meant to be read. Our AI obituary writer handles the notice.

Is this eulogy writer free?

Yes. 100% free with no accounts and no signup required.

Will the eulogy sound personal or generic?

That depends on your input. Real specifics make it sound like your person. Edit in your own voice before the service.

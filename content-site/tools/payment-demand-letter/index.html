What actually works

Tone ladder

Friendly → firm → final. Jumping straight to threats burns bridges and can look bad if you later need a judge. Match mode to how many prior contacts you already made.

One clear ask

Every demand letter needs amount, reference, original due date, and a new pay-by date. Ambiguity is why invoices age.

Only real next steps

Never invent lawsuits, “collections tomorrow,” or criminal claims. State only actions you are willing and able to take.

Keep the trail

Save invoices, SOWs, and each reminder. Small claims and collections start with documents, not volume of anger.

Real ways people use a demand letter

Freelancer unpaid invoice

The most common case: project delivered, invoice overdue 30+ days, two casual reminders ignored. Send the firm follow-up with the invoice attached and a 10–14 day deadline — most non-paying clients settle at this stage and never reach court.

Contractor and trade debts

Use the final notice when a client stalls on a completed job. Reference the contract or change order, the original due date, and the exact next step you will take — filing in small claims or referring to collections — and only state steps you are genuinely prepared to take.

Personal and roommate debts

Shared rent, utilities, or a loan to a friend do not need legal language, but they still need a dated record. The personal mode keeps it factual — what was agreed, what is unpaid, the total — so the conversation stays about numbers, not accusations.

Landlord and tenant amounts

Late rent, unpaid damage charges, or a deposit dispute benefit from the same structure: amount, reference to the lease, prior reminders with dates, and one clear pay-by date. A paper trail is what wins disputes with housing offices and courts alike.

FAQ

Is what I type private?

Your input is sent to our AI service to generate your result. No account, no signup, and nothing is linked to your identity. We count anonymous usage per tool for analytics. Your text is never sold.

Will this guarantee payment?

No. A clear paper trail improves odds and prepares you if you escalate.

Should I use certified mail?

Useful for final notices and larger amounts. Email is fine for friendly reminders if that is how you already work.

Late fees?

Only charge late fees or interest if your contract or invoice terms allow them — in the US, courts generally will not enforce a penalty the client never agreed to, though some states have prompt-payment laws that add statutory interest. In the UK, business-to-business debts get automatic statutory interest and compensation whether or not your contract mentions it. Don’t invent penalties.

How long should I wait before sending a demand letter?

Send it after normal follow-ups have failed — a common rule of thumb is 30+ days overdue with three or more ignored reminders. Sending a formal demand too early can look disproportionate and weaken its credibility, so match the tone to how many contacts you have already made.

What should I do if they ignore the letter?

Put the deadline in your calendar and wait. Do not send more informal follow-ups — the letter set the terms. Most debtors who were going to pay settle at the demand-letter stage. If the deadline passes, follow through on the next step you stated: small claims filing, a collections referral, or stopping work.

Is this legal advice?

No. For large amounts or disputes, consult a lawyer or small-claims resources in your area.

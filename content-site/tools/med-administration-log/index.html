Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Medication Administration Record (MAR)?

A MAR is a professional grid sheet used by nurses and caregivers to document every medication dose given to a patient. Accurate recording prevents double-dosing and ensures medication compliance.

How does the drug interaction audit work?

The AI model reviews the active medications, dosages, and routes listed on your MAR grid, references standard clinical safety criteria, and alerts you to possible drug-drug interactions or side-effect risks.

Importance of MAR and Handoff Sheets

In senior care, memory care, and group home environments, medication administration errors are among the most common and preventable safety hazards. A Medication Administration Record (MAR) is the primary line of defense. By checking off administrations in real time, caregivers prevent double-dosing, missed medications, or scheduling anomalies. In addition to a structured log, a descriptive caregiver shift handoff note ensures that oncoming staff members are aware of vital signs (like blood pressure or blood sugar), refusals, and any behavioral symptoms that occurred after a medication was given.

Common Drug-Drug Interactions to Monitor

Caregivers must remain alert to dangerous drug interactions. For example, administering multiple medications that lower blood pressure (like beta-blockers and ACE inhibitors) can lead to severe orthostatic hypotension and increased fall risks, especially in elderly patients. Anticholinergic drugs taken alongside cognitive enhancers (such as acetylcholinesterase inhibitors like Donepezil) can cancel out the effects of both medications, leading to worsened confusion or memory loss. The AI audit highlights these safety profiles to ensure they are reported to the prescribing physician.

Best Practices for Caregiver Communications

When writing handoff notes for the next shift, focus on objective, clear, and actionable data. Avoid subjective comments like "patient was difficult." Instead, write: "Patient refused morning dose of Metoprolol at 08:00, stating they felt too dizzy. BP checked: 102/60. Notification sent to family representative and doctor's office at 08:30." Ensure all vitals are recorded alongside any PRN (as-needed) medications given, including the reason for administration and the patient's subsequent response.

How to Use This Tool in 5 Steps

1. Add each medication

Enter the name and dosage, then pick the route, frequency, and scheduled time. Check the list against the prescription bottles or the discharge paperwork before you start, not from memory.

2. Sign off each dose as you give it

Tap the Morning, Afternoon, Evening, or Night box for that medication right when the dose is given. The tool stamps the current time, so the next person on shift can see at a glance what was already given and what is still due.

3. Add shift notes

Record refusals, missed doses, PRN doses with the reason given, and any vitals or symptoms you noticed. Write facts with times, not opinions: "Refused morning Metoprolol at 08:00, said dizzy. BP 102/60."

4. Pick an audit type and generate

Choose Drug Safety Audit to flag possible interactions and side-effect risks, or Handoff Summary for a next-shift briefing. Click "Audit Interactions & Build Handoff" to generate the report.

5. Print it or send it to the shift report

Use Print / PDF to file a paper copy in the care binder, or use the handoff button to send the MAR and your notes straight into the Caregiver Shift Report tool. Your medication list stays saved in this browser for the next visit.

When to Use This Log

Family caregiving at home

One person handles mornings, another handles evenings. The grid stops the "did anyone give Dad his pill?" argument.

CNA and home health shifts

Sign off doses per shift and hand the next aide a printed MAR plus a written handoff note instead of a verbal summary.

Memory care and dementia care

Patients who cannot report what they took need a written record. The timestamped grid is the proof of what was given and when.

After a hospital discharge

New prescriptions and changed doses are where errors happen. Enter the discharge list on day one and run the safety audit before the first week is over.

Doctor and pharmacist visits

Bring a printed MAR showing actual doses given, refusals, and PRN use. It answers the "is the medication working?" question with real data.

Tracking side effects

Log dizziness, drowsiness, or appetite changes next to the dose that preceded them. Patterns like that are what a prescriber needs to adjust a dose.

This tool is a documentation aid, not medical advice. The safety audit flags possible issues for you to raise with a pharmacist or prescriber. It does not replace professional review, and it never tells you to start, stop, or change a dose.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is what I type private?

Your input is sent to our AI service to generate your result. No account, no signup, and nothing is linked to your identity. We count anonymous usage per tool for analytics. Your text is never sold. Your medication list itself is stored in your browser, not on our servers.

What is a Medication Administration Record (MAR)?

A MAR is a professional grid sheet used by nurses and caregivers to document every medication dose given to a patient. Accurate recording prevents double-dosing and ensures medication compliance.

How does the drug interaction audit work?

The AI model reviews the active medications, dosages, and routes listed on your MAR grid, references standard clinical safety criteria, and alerts you to possible drug-drug interactions or side-effect risks.

Can I print my completed MAR and report?

Yes. Clicking the "Print / PDF" button will format the MAR grid and completed handoff notes into a clean, printer-friendly layout suitable for physical care files or medical binder inserts.

Where is my medication list stored?

In your browser, using local storage on your own device. It stays there between visits on the same browser and is never uploaded to our servers. Clearing your browser data deletes it, so print a copy if the list matters. When you run an audit or handoff, the medication list and your notes are sent to our AI service to generate that report.

Is this a substitute for a pharmacist or doctor?

No. The audit is a screening aid that flags possible interactions and risks for you to raise with a pharmacist or prescriber. It is not medical advice, and it never tells you to start, stop, or change a dose. Always verify changes with the prescribing clinician.

Can I use it for more than one person?

One person per list. The tool keeps a single medication list in your browser, so do not mix two patients in one grid. Finish and print one person's MAR, clear the list, then start the next person's. Mixing patients is how doses get given to the wrong person.

What should I do if a dose is missed or refused?

Record it in the shift notes with the time and the reason, and leave that shift box unchecked so the next caregiver can see it was not given. Never double up a missed dose unless the prescriber or pharmacist says to. Tell the next shift and, for a refused dose, tell the family contact or the care agency.

Is this tool HIPAA compliant?

No, it is a free web tool, not a certified medical record system. Your medication list is stored in your browser, and the audit sends that list to our AI service, so do not enter full names, dates of birth, or other patient identifiers. Check your facility or agency policy before using it for work records.

Can I send this log to the Caregiver Shift Report tool?

Yes. After you generate an audit or handoff, the handoff button below the report sends your MAR, your shift notes, and the audit summary into the Caregiver Shift Report tool, where it becomes the starting point for a full shift report.

Importance of MAR and Handoff Sheets

In senior care, memory care, and group home environments, medication administration errors are among the most common and preventable safety hazards. A Medication Administration Record (MAR) is the primary line of defense. By checking off administrations in real time, caregivers prevent double-dosing, missed medications, or scheduling anomalies. In addition to a structured log, a descriptive caregiver shift handoff note ensures that oncoming staff members are aware of vital signs (like blood pressure or blood sugar), refusals, and any behavioral symptoms that occurred after a medication was given.

Common Drug-Drug Interactions to Monitor

Caregivers must remain alert to dangerous drug interactions. For example, administering multiple medications that lower blood pressure (like beta-blockers and ACE inhibitors) can lead to severe orthostatic hypotension and increased fall risks, especially in elderly patients. Anticholinergic drugs taken alongside cognitive enhancers (such as acetylcholinesterase inhibitors like Donepezil) can cancel out the effects of both medications, leading to worsened confusion or memory loss. The AI audit highlights these safety profiles to ensure they are reported to the prescribing physician.

Best Practices for Caregiver Communications

When writing handoff notes for the next shift, focus on objective, clear, and actionable data. Avoid subjective comments like "patient was difficult." Instead, write: "Patient refused morning dose of Metoprolol at 08:00, stating they felt too dizzy. BP checked: 102/60. Notification sent to family representative and doctor's office at 08:30." Ensure all vitals are recorded alongside any PRN (as-needed) medications given, including the reason for administration and the patient's subsequent response.

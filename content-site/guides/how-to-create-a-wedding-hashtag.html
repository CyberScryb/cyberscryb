How to Create a Wedding Hashtag

Pun formulas, the one-hashtag rule, and how to check yours is not taken — before your guests' photos land in someone else's album.

By CyberScryb 📅 Updated October 10, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read
⚡ Key Takeaways (TL;DR)

Pick ONE hashtag: short, easy to spell, unique enough to own. Mine your last names for puns, fall back on your story (how you met, the venue, shared jokes) when names do not cooperate, and search it on Instagram and TikTok before you commit.

The Pun Formulas That Actually Work

Nearly every good wedding hashtag is one of a handful of formulas. [LastName] + [wedding word]: #BakerFinallyMetzger, #ToHaveAndToHolden. Rhyme: #BestDayWithBailey. The year: #TheSmiths2027. The punny verb: #SheSaidYesToJess, #HappilyEverEverson. Start with last names — they carry the most wordplay — then try first names, then the story. Write twenty fast without judging. The keeper is usually hiding in there by number fifteen.

The One-Hashtag Rule

One official hashtag. Not one for the ceremony and one for the reception, not one from each of you, not a backup. Multiple hashtags split your photo album across feeds, guests never remember which one to use, and half your photos become unfindable. Decide together, then commit: put it on the invitations, a sign at the ceremony entrance, cards on the reception tables, and in the DJ's announcements. Guests will not remember a hashtag they saw once.

Check It Is Not Taken — Before You Print Anything

Search your candidate as an exact hashtag on Instagram and TikTok. If another couple's wedding already owns it, your photos will land in their album — which is awkward for everyone. Common name combos (#SmithWedding2026) are almost always taken; that is exactly what the pun formulas are for. Also say it out loud and have someone spell it back to you. If a guest cannot spell it after hearing it once, it will not get used.

When Names Do Not Cooperate

Some names just do not pun. That is fine — the story is usually better anyway. Where you met, the venue, the date digits, a shared hobby or inside joke: #SwipedRightOnSarah, #BarnRaisedAndBound, #TakeTwoForTaylor. If you want zero cleverness risk, the classic is always safe: both first names plus the year (#MayaAndJordan2027). Clean, searchable, and nobody cringes at it in ten years.

What to Avoid

  • Hashtags so long nobody will type them after two drinks
  • Words that run together into an unfortunate reading — say it aloud
  • Special characters or numbers in confusing spots (0 vs O)
  • all-lowercase — capitalize each word so it reads at a glance
  • Announcing it after the wedding instead of before

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I create a wedding hashtag?

Start with your names — especially last names, where the best puns hide. Add story details, apply a pun formula, and keep it short, spellable, and unique.

How do I check if a wedding hashtag is taken?

Search it as an exact hashtag on Instagram and TikTok before you commit. If someone else's wedding owns it, pick the next option.

How many wedding hashtags should we have?

One official hashtag. Multiple hashtags split your album across feeds and guests forget which to use.

What if our names do not make good puns?

Use the story — where you met, the venue, shared jokes — or go classic with both names and the year.

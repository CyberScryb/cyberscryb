Stop Paying for Things Free AI Does Better

You're paying monthly for stuff a free AI tool does in ten seconds. Here's the uncomfortable truth about the subscription trap, and the seven things to audit today.

The uncomfortable truth

Here's the uncomfortable truth: you're probably paying $30, $50, maybe $100 a month for software that a free AI tool replaces in ten seconds. Not because the paid app is better. Because you signed up two years ago, forgot about it, and the charge is small enough to ignore. That's not a subscription. That's a leak. And leaks are how $600 a year disappears without you noticing.

The seven things to audit

Pull three months of statements. Look for these:

  • Resume and cover letter builders. $15-30/month for what a free AI writer does better, because it actually knows your background.
  • Paraphrasing and grammar subscriptions. The free tier of most of these is a demo. A free AI rephraser does the whole job.
  • Social media caption tools. $10-20/month to generate captions. AI writes them free, in your voice, in seconds.
  • Meeting notes summarizers you barely use. If you open it twice a month, it's not worth $12. Paste the notes into a free summarizer instead.
  • Stock photo subscriptions for blog images. For casual use, free AI image tools and free stock sites cover it.
  • "AI assistants" you forgot you bought. Half the apps added an AI upsell last year. Check if you're paying for the same capability twice.
  • Anything with a free tier you never downgraded from. The oldest trick in SaaS: the paid plan you meant to cancel.

The two-week test

Don't cancel blind. Run the free tool alongside the paid one for two weeks. Same tasks, both tools. If the free one covers your actual use, cancel with confidence. If you hit a wall, you just learned exactly what the paid version is worth, which is more than most subscribers ever figure out. Either way you win.

What to keep paying for

This isn't "cancel everything." Keep paying for specialized workflows, team collaboration, integrations with your other software, and anything business-critical where downtime costs real money. The rule: pay for the workflow, not the writing. The writing part is free now. That's not an opinion, that's just what the market did.

FAQ

Can free AI tools really replace paid apps?

For writing, drafting, summarizing, and one-off tasks, usually yes. Paid apps still win for specialized workflows, collaboration, and anything needing integrations. But most people pay for the writing part, and that's exactly what AI does free.

What's the catch with free AI tools?

The usual ones: usage limits, your prompts may train future models, and no human support. For personal one-off tasks, none of that matters much. For business-critical workflows, keep the paid tool.

How do I find which subscriptions I'm still paying for?

Pull three months of bank and card statements and highlight every recurring charge. Most people find two or three they've forgotten about. Cancel first, ask questions later; you can always resubscribe.

Is it safe to cancel and rely on free tools?

Try before you cancel. Run the free tool alongside the paid one for two weeks. If the free one covers your actual use, cancel with confidence. If you hit a wall, you learned exactly what the paid version is worth.

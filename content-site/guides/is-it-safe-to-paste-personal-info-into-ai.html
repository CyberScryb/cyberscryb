Is It Safe to Paste Your Personal Info Into an AI Tool?

That chat box is not a diary. Here's the uncomfortable truth about where your words go, the six things to never paste, and how to anonymize anything in ten seconds.

By CyberScryb 📅 Updated October 31, 2026 ⏱ 4 min read
⚡ Key Takeaways (TL;DR)

Assume anything you paste into an AI tool could be stored and seen by someone else someday. Never share SSNs, account numbers, passwords, addresses, kids' details, or named medical info. Anonymize first: swap names for roles. Browser-based tools that run client-side never send your text anywhere.

The uncomfortable truth

Here's the uncomfortable truth: that friendly chat box is not a diary, and it's not a locked room. Most AI companies store what you type, at least for a while, and plenty of them train future models on it unless you dig through settings to opt out. So the only sane rule is this: assume anything you paste could be read by a stranger someday, and type accordingly.

The six things to never paste

Burn this list into your brain. Never share:

  • Social Security numbers. Obviously. Don't even test it.
  • Bank account or card numbers. No exceptions.
  • Passwords. Not even "is this a strong password" with the real one.
  • Your home address. City is fine. Street number isn't.
  • Your kids' names, ages, or schools. Ever.
  • Medical details tied to your name. "What does this lab result mean" is fine. Your name plus your diagnosis isn't.

The test is simple: if losing control of it would hurt you, don't paste it. When in doubt, leave it out.

How to anonymize anything in ten seconds

You can still get help with personal situations. You just swap identifiers for roles before you paste. "My landlord, John Smith" becomes "my landlord." "My 8-year-old son Jake" becomes "my kid." "I was fired from Acme Corp on March 3rd" becomes "I was fired from my job last month." The AI doesn't need the names to help with the situation. It needs the shape of the problem, not the ID numbers attached to it.

Know the difference: server vs browser

Here's the part nobody explains. Most AI tools send your text to a company's server. But some tools run entirely in your browser: the processing happens on your machine and your words never leave it. That's a real, meaningful difference. When a tool says it runs client-side, there's nothing to store, nothing to leak, nothing to train on. For anything sensitive, that's the kind of tool you want.

FAQ

Do AI companies store what I type?

Most of them keep it, at least for a while, and many use conversations to train future models unless you opt out. Treat anything you paste as potentially permanent and potentially seen by someone else someday.

What should I never paste into an AI tool?

Social Security numbers, bank account and card numbers, passwords, your home address, your kids' names and schools, and medical details tied to your name. If losing control of it would hurt you, don't paste it.

Can I still get help with personal situations?

Yes, just anonymize first. Swap names for roles: my landlord instead of John Smith, my kid instead of a name and age. The AI doesn't need the identifiers to help with the situation.

Are browser-based AI tools safer?

Tools that run entirely in your browser never send your text to a server, so there's nothing to store or leak. That's a meaningful difference. When a tool says it runs client-side, your words stay on your machine.

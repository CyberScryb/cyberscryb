The uncomfortable truth about repair requests

Here's the uncomfortable truth: that phone call where you told your landlord about the leak? Legally, it barely happened. Oral requests are easy to forget, easy to deny, and worthless if things escalate. A dated letter with photos referenced by name is a record. Records win at housing agencies and in small claims. Phone calls don't. Put it in writing, every single time.

What goes in the letter

Keep it factual and complete. Unit address and your name. A clear description of the issue, no drama, just facts. When it started. Every prior oral notice with dates ("I called about this on March 3rd and again on March 17th"). Photos referenced by filename. How they can access the unit for repairs. And a clear ask with a reasonable deadline, for example: "please complete the repair within 14 days." That deadline matters. It starts the clock that everything else runs on.

Deposits and habitability

Two different fights, two different playbooks. For deposit demands, use our Security Deposit Demand tool with statute references for your state. Deadlines and penalties vary wildly by state, so the statute matters more than the tone. For habitability issues, the broken heater, the sewage backup, the lock that doesn't lock, stick to facts and cite local code if you have it. And skip the threats in the first letter. Threats make landlords defensive. Facts with a deadline make them act. Save the teeth for letter two.

The paper trail habit

One letter is good. A habit is better. Every request in writing. Every response saved. Photos dated. If it ever goes to a housing agency or a courtroom, the tenant with the folder wins. Start the folder now, before you need it.

FAQ

Should I email or certified mail?

Email for speed plus a PDF copy. For serious habitability issues, also send certified mail so delivery is documented. Belt and suspenders.

Can a template replace local legal advice?

No. Templates and AI drafts help you organize facts. Local tenant law varies wildly, so check legal aid for your city if the landlord ignores written notice. Many legal aid offices handle housing free.

How long should I give them to fix it?

Fourteen days is the common reasonable window for non-emergencies. For habitability emergencies, no heat, no water, sewage, broken locks, the window is days, not weeks, and most states say so explicitly. Name the deadline in the letter either way.

What if they retaliate after I complain in writing?

Retaliation for asserting your rights is illegal in most states, and your paper trail is exactly what proves the timing. Keep every letter, every response, every date. The folder is your shield.

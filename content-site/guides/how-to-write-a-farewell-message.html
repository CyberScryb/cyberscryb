How to Write a Farewell Message

One specific memory, genuine thanks, one forward-looking line. Examples for a coworker, your boss, and your whole team, plus what never to write.

By CyberScryb 📅 Updated October 13, 2026 ⏱ 6 min read
⚡ Key Takeaways (TL;DR)

A good farewell message has three parts: one specific memory that proves the relationship was real, genuine thanks tied to something they actually did, and one forward-looking line with a way to stay in touch. Keep it under 100 words for a card, under 250 for an email. Never include negativity about the company or promises you will not keep.

The 3 Parts of a Farewell Message People Remember

Most farewell messages are interchangeable. Swap the names and "Wishing you all the best in your future endeavors!" works for anyone, which is exactly why nobody remembers it. The messages people keep — the ones that get screenshotted and saved — all share the same three-part shape:

  1. One specific memory. A project, a habit, a joke, a hard week you survived together. "I will never forget the night we stayed until 2am to save the launch" proves the relationship was real. This one detail does more work than everything else combined.
  2. Genuine thanks for something concrete. Not "thanks for everything" — thanks for the thing. The mentorship on the client calls, the cover during the rough quarter, the way they made Mondays bearable. Tie the thanks to an action and it lands.
  3. One forward-looking line, with a door left open. Wish them well at what is next, and if you mean it, give them a way to reach you. "They are lucky to have you" plus a real invitation beats a vague "let's stay in touch" that nobody believes.

Example: Farewell Message to a Coworker

Warm, personal, the length of a good card or Slack message:

Priya, I am really going to miss having you two desks over. Three years of surviving holiday season together, your emergency donut runs on the rough Mondays, and that time you covered my shift without being asked when my kid was sick — that is the stuff I will actually remember. You made this team better every single day. They have no idea how lucky they are. Do not be a stranger — coffee is on me whenever you are back in town.

Example: Farewell Message to Your Boss

Professional, grateful, safe for a company-wide channel:

Marcus, as I head out on Friday I wanted to say thank you properly. The way you ran this team — clear expectations, honest feedback, and actually defending our time upstairs — taught me more about leadership than any book could. I am a better engineer for having worked for you. I hope our paths cross again, and my door is always open if you ever need a reference in reverse.

Example: Farewell Message to Your Whole Team (You Are Leaving)

The last-day email. Grateful, specific, forward-looking — this is the message that keeps the door open for references and future roles:

Team — Friday is my last day, and I wanted to say this properly instead of just vanishing from Slack. These past four years taught me more than I can fit in an email: the launch marathons, the whiteboard arguments that somehow always made the work better, and the way this group shows up for each other when it counts. Thank you for all of it. I am excited about what is next, and I am rooting for every one of you. You know where to find me — my personal email is in the signature, and I mean it when I say let us stay in touch.

What NOT to Write

  • Company negativity. "Finally escaping this place!" might get a laugh in the moment, but it lives in writing forever — and it insults everyone who is staying.
  • Gossip about the departure. Do not speculate about why someone is leaving, who pushed them out, or who is replacing them. Not your story to tell.
  • Backhanded compliments. "Good luck — you will need it over there" is not funny in writing. If the joke needs your tone of voice to land, it does not belong in a message.
  • Promises you will not keep. "Let us get coffee every week!" is a lie you tell to feel better about leaving. Say "let us stay in touch" only if you will actually reply.
  • The generic template. If your message works with any name swapped in, rewrite it. One specific memory fixes this every time.

Email vs. Slack vs. Card

Match the channel to the relationship. A quick Slack or Teams message fits a close coworker — it is where the relationship lived. An email fits a team-wide goodbye or a message to a manager, because it is findable later and feels deliberate. A handwritten card still wins for retirements and mentors; it is the only format anyone keeps in a drawer. Whatever the channel, timing matters: send it before the last day, not a week after.

Farewell Message vs. Farewell Speech

A message is written and short. A speech is spoken aloud at a going-away gathering and runs a few minutes, with an opening, a couple of stories, and a toast-style close. If you have been asked to say a few words in person, a message will not cut it — read our guide on how to write a farewell speech instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I write in a farewell message to a coworker?

One specific memory that proves the relationship was real, genuine thanks tied to something they actually did, and one forward-looking line with a way to stay in touch. Specificity is the whole game: "I will miss our 8am debugging sessions" beats "you were a great colleague" every time.

How long should a farewell message be?

Fifty to 100 words for a card or Slack message; 150 to 250 words for an email to a team or a manager. Longer than that and it stops being a message and starts being a speech.

What is the difference between a farewell message and a farewell speech?

A message is written and short — a card, a Slack note, an email. A speech is spoken aloud at a going-away gathering, runs a few minutes, and has an opening, stories, and a toast-style close. If you have been asked to say a few words in person, you need a speech, not a message.

What should you never say in a farewell message?

Nothing negative about the company or the departure, no gossip about replacements, no backhanded compliments, and no promises to stay in touch that you will not keep. Keep the focus on the person, not the politics.

Should I send a farewell message on my last day or before?

Before, or on the last day at the latest. A goodbye that arrives a week late reads as an afterthought. If you are the one leaving, send your team message on your second-to-last day so people have time to reply.

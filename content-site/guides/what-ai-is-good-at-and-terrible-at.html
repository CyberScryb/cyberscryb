What AI Is Actually Good At (and Terrible At)

People either expect miracles or garbage from AI. Both are wrong. Here are the five things it's genuinely great at and the five it's terrible at, so you stop giving it the wrong jobs.

By CyberScryb 📅 Updated October 31, 2026 ⏱ 5 min read
⚡ Key Takeaways (TL;DR)

Great at: drafting, brainstorming, explaining, summarizing, rephrasing. Terrible at: verified facts, real-time info, exact math, genuine judgment, and anything where being wrong costs you. Match the job to the machine and it stops disappointing you.

The uncomfortable truth

Here's the uncomfortable truth: most AI disappointment isn't the machine's fault. It's yours, for handing a prediction engine a job that needs a fact-checker, a calculator, or a professional license. AI is a brilliant specialist with a very narrow band of genius. Use it inside the band and it feels like magic. Use it outside the band and it feels like a confident idiot. The band is the whole game.

Five things it's genuinely great at

  • Drafting. First versions of anything written. The blank page is the enemy, and AI kills it in ten seconds.
  • Brainstorming. Twenty ideas in a minute. Most will be mediocre. Three will be worth stealing. That's the point.
  • Explaining. "Explain this like I'm twelve" works on almost anything. It reframes until it clicks.
  • Summarizing. Long text in, short version out. Meeting notes, articles, threads, docs.
  • Rephrasing. Shorter, funnier, more formal, less likely to start a fight. Tone-shifting is its home turf.

Five things it's terrible at

  • Verified facts. It invents dates, names, and numbers with total confidence. Never trust, always check.
  • Anything current. News, prices, schedules. Its training has a cutoff. The world doesn't.
  • Exact math. It predicts words, not numbers. Fine for explaining the method, dangerous for the final figure.
  • Real judgment. Taste, ethics, "should I do this." It'll give you an answer, but it has no skin in the game and no values. That's your job.
  • Anything where being wrong costs you. Medical, legal, financial, safety. The stakes are yours. Don't outsource them to a guesser.

The rule

Before you type, ask one question: is this a creating job or a knowing job? Creating: drafts, ideas, explanations, rephrases. AI all day. Knowing: facts, figures, what's true right now, what you should do. That's you, plus a search engine, plus maybe a professional. Get that split right and AI stops being disappointing. It was never broken. It was just misassigned.

FAQ

What is AI best used for?

Drafting, brainstorming, explaining, summarizing, and rephrasing. Anything where a fast first version from a fluent assistant saves you real time and you stay in charge of the final call.

What should I never use AI for?

Anything where being wrong costs you and you can't verify: medical decisions, legal deadlines, financial moves, and facts you can't check. Also anything needing real-time info, genuine creativity with taste, or actual accountability.

Can AI replace professional advice?

No. It can help you understand the landscape and prepare good questions, which makes the professional's time more useful. But it can't be liable, it can't know your full situation, and it invents facts. The expert stays.

Why is AI bad at math?

Because it predicts words, not numbers. It can walk through math problems step by step and often gets them right, but it has no calculator inside. For anything where the number has to be exact, use an actual calculator and let AI explain the method.

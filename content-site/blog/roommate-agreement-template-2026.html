The Clauses Every Roommate Agreement Needs (And Why Each One Is There)

Published October 12, 2026 · 8 min read · Roommate Guide

A roommate agreement template is only useful if every clause earns its place. Each section below exists because someone, somewhere, had a real fight about exactly that thing. Here is what goes in, clause by clause, and the dispute each one is designed to prevent.

1. The Parties and the Address

Full legal names of every roommate and the full rental address, including unit number. This sounds obvious, but it is the clause that makes the rest of the document mean something: every obligation below attaches to a named person at a named place. Leave a dated line for each signature at the end of the document, not just at the top. The dispute this prevents: "I never agreed to that," said by the person whose name appears nowhere.

2. The Rent Split, With Actual Numbers

Not the method. The numbers. "Rent is $1,800 per month. Alex pays $722, Jordan pays $565, Sam pays $513, calculated by square footage." A clause that says "split fairly" is a clause that will be argued about. Dollar amounts are not arguable; they can only be honored or broken. The dispute this prevents: the month-three realization that one person has been paying for a bedroom they are not getting. If you need help picking a method, our rent splitting guide runs the math four ways with worked examples.

3. Payment Schedule and Due Date

When rent is due to the landlord, when each roommate pays the person who sends it, and how. "Rent is due to the landlord on the 1st. Each roommate pays their share to Alex by the 28th of the previous month via bank transfer." The classic failure: one roommate "sends it on the 1st" and the landlord charges a late fee, and now three people are arguing about who owes the $75. The dispute this prevents: late fees, and the slow resentment of the roommate who always covers for the one who pays "when they get around to it."

4. Utilities: Which Ones, How Split, Who Pays

Three questions, all answered: which utilities are shared (name them), how they split (evenly is the default; it tracks people, not bedroom size), and who pays the provider with what reimbursement deadline. "Electric, gas, water, internet, and trash are split evenly. Jordan holds the accounts; the others reimburse within 7 days of receiving the bill." The dispute this prevents: the first $240 winter electric bill, and the Venmo request that sits unpaid for six weeks while Jordan quietly builds a grudge.

5. Guest and Overnight Policy

The clause that ends more living situations than any other. Set a real number: "Overnight guests are welcome up to 2 nights per week per person with same-day notice. A guest staying more than 3 nights per week for two consecutive weeks counts as an additional occupant, and the household will renegotiate rent and utilities." The dispute this prevents: the partner who slowly moves in, uses everything, pays nothing, and turns the other roommates into unwilling landlords. Name the trigger before it happens, because nobody negotiates fairly in the middle of it.

6. Quiet Hours and Shared Space Rules

"Quiet hours are 10 PM to 8 AM on weeknights. Headphones required in common areas after 10 PM." Add the specific friction points of your household: shower schedules on work mornings, kitchen use during someone's work-from-home calls, TV volume. The dispute this prevents: the slow-burn war between the 6 AM gym person and the midnight gamer. It is not about who is right. It is about writing down what "reasonable" means before anyone is tired.

7. Cleaning: Who, What, How Often

"Cleaning rotates weekly: kitchen, bathroom, and common areas. Each person cleans their assigned zone by Sunday evening. Personal dishes are washed same-day." Vague cleaning expectations are where passive aggression lives: the note left on the counter, the trash "forgotten" for the third week running. The dispute this prevents: every small one. A rotation turns "you never clean" into "it is your week," which is a fact instead of an accusation.

8. Move-Out, Notice, and the Empty Room

"Any roommate may leave with 30 days written notice. The departing roommate is responsible for their share of rent through the notice period and for helping find a suitable replacement, subject to landlord approval. The security deposit is split equally and returned by the landlord per the lease; roommates settle any damage claims between themselves before move-out." The dispute this prevents: the roommate who vanishes mid-lease and leaves two people covering a missing third of the rent while they scramble. Decide the exit rules on a calm day, when everyone is still friends.

The Signing Checklist

An unsigned agreement is a draft, not a document. Before you call it done:

  1. Read it aloud together. Out loud catches the clause everyone "thought" said something else.
  2. Fill every blank. No "[INSERT DUE DATE]" left unfilled. Blanks are future arguments.
  3. Everyone signs and dates. Printed name, signature, date. No signature, no agreement.
  4. Each roommate keeps a copy. Photo of the signed pages in everyone's phone, plus the originals somewhere findable.
  5. Set a review date. Six months out, or whenever something changes: a partner moving in, a job loss, a room swap. Agreements rot when life changes and the paper does not.
